# Module 17 — Psychology & Risk Management

**Est. length:** 3 days (6 hours) to build the systems; both are lived daily afterward.
**Prerequisite:** Module 16 (journal data feeds this directly). **Feeds into:** Module 18 (paper trading tests these under real conditions), Module 20–21 (playbooks).

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn

Crypto- and meme-coin-specific psychological failure modes (FOMO, chasing pumps, greed, fear, revenge trading, overtrading, emotional attachment to a coin, influencer dependence, confirmation bias, narrative bias, fear of missing a 10x/100x, gambling mentality, holding losers on hope); and risk management as a core discipline — position sizing, risk per trade, stops, risk/reward, maximum daily/weekly loss, drawdown, losing streaks, slippage risk, liquidity risk, tail risk, contract/smart-contract risk — with meme coins requiring materially stricter controls than BTC/ETH.

## 2. Why It Matters

Your framework (Module 15) and your data (Module 16) are only as good as your ability to actually follow them under real pressure. Meme coins specifically manufacture the exact psychological conditions (extreme speed, extreme FOMO, social proof, near-gambling payout structure) that break discipline fastest — this module exists because knowing the framework isn't the same as executing it at 2am when a token is up 400% in an hour.

## 3. Crypto & Meme-Coin Psychology

- **FOMO (fear of missing out) and chasing pumps:** entering after a large move has already happened, driven by the discomfort of "missing it" rather than a validated setup. **Countermeasure:** your Module 15 framework's entry criteria exist precisely to remove "it's already pumping" as a valid reason to enter.
- **Greed and the 10x/100x fantasy:** meme coins' occasional enormous winners create a specific psychological trap — anchoring your expectations/behavior to the rare outsized outcome rather than the realistic, base-rate distribution (most meme coins go to near-zero). **Countermeasure:** size every position as if it will likely go to zero (because most do), and treat any large win as a pleasant surprise, not the expected case.
- **Fear (of a real loss) causing premature exits:** cutting a validated setup early out of anxiety, degrading a real edge through poor execution (Module 7 §5's execution-quality tracking exists to catch this).
- **Revenge trading:** re-entering (often larger, faster, less validated) immediately after a loss, trying to "win it back." **Countermeasure:** a hard rule — no new trade within a defined cooldown period after a loss, and never increase size to recover a loss.
- **Overtrading:** taking marginal or invalid setups out of restlessness or a need for action, especially common when scrolling meme-coin trending lists for hours. **Countermeasure:** your Module 15 qualification/no-trade-condition stages, applied without exception.
- **Emotional attachment to a coin:** treating a token you hold as "your" project, becoming resistant to selling because you've built a narrative/identity around holding it, rather than evaluating it fresh each day. **Countermeasure:** re-run the Module 13 checklist and Module 15 invalidation criteria on your *own* holdings periodically, as if you were considering it fresh.
- **Influencer dependence:** outsourcing your decision-making to a specific account's calls rather than your own validated process (directly related to Module 11's credibility work). **Countermeasure:** treat any influencer call the same as any other discovery-stage lead — it still has to pass your full framework.
- **Confirmation bias and narrative bias:** once you've decided you like a token, unconsciously weighting new information that supports holding/buying more heavily than information that contradicts it. **Countermeasure:** explicitly look for disconfirming evidence (a "devil's advocate" pass) before every entry and before holding through a red flag.
- **Gambling mentality:** meme-coin trading can psychologically resemble gambling — fast, high-variance, social, dopamine-driven — more than it resembles disciplined trading. Recognizing this explicitly (rather than pretending it's "just trading") is itself a protective step; if you notice you're chasing the feeling of a bet rather than executing a validated process, that's a signal to stop for the session.
- **Holding losers on hope:** refusing to exit at your predefined invalidation because "it might come back," turning a defined-risk trade into an undefined one. **Countermeasure:** invalidation levels/conditions are set *before* entry specifically so they don't get renegotiated emotionally after entry.

## 4. Risk Management — Core Discipline

- **Position sizing and risk per trade:** the same discipline from your ICT futures training — define % risk per trade before entry, based on your stop distance, not a fixed dollar/coin amount.
- **Stops and invalidation:** every trade has a predefined exit condition — for meme coins, both a price-based stop *and* a condition-based invalidation (Module 15 §Stage 6), since a rug/security event can invalidate a thesis instantly, faster than a price alert might trigger.
- **Risk/reward:** same principle as futures — know your planned R:R before entry; for meme coins, factor in that slippage on exit (see below) often makes your realized R worse than your planned R, especially on size.
- **Maximum daily/weekly loss limits:** set separately for BTC/ETH and for meme coins (they should not share one pool logically, since meme-coin variance is categorically higher) — hitting the limit ends trading for that period, no exceptions, no "one more."
- **Drawdown and losing streaks:** know, from your Module 16 journal stats, what a realistic losing streak looks like for your process, so a real one doesn't trigger panic-driven deviation from the plan.
- **Slippage risk:** on meme coins especially, your *realized* exit price on a real-sized position can be meaningfully worse than the last-traded price shown on the chart, because your own sell order moves a thin pool (Module 3). Factor this into position sizing — a size that "looks fine" on-chart can be genuinely hard to exit at a fair price.
- **Liquidity risk:** the risk that you simply cannot exit at a reasonable price at all if liquidity is pulled or dries up — this is a hard ceiling on how large a meme-coin position should ever be, independent of your conviction level.
- **Tail risk:** the realistic probability-weighted outcome for any single meme coin includes a meaningful chance of a near-total loss (rug, exploit, abandonment) — size every position so that outcome, if it happens, doesn't meaningfully damage your account or your ability to keep trading.
- **Contract/smart-contract risk:** even a token that passes every check in Module 13 can still have an undiscovered vulnerability exploited later, or a legitimate project's contract can be hacked — this is a real, irreducible risk category, not just "scam avoidance," and it argues for genuinely small position sizing on top of scam-avoidance diligence, not instead of it.
- **Know your maximum potential loss before entering — always:** for every position, state explicitly, before entry, "if this goes to zero right now, I lose $X, which is Y% of my account, and that is acceptable to me." If you can't say that honestly, the position is too large.

## 5. Practical Exercises

- Write your personal position-sizing rules: max % risk per trade for BTC/ETH, max % risk per trade for meme coins (smaller), max concurrent meme-coin positions, max daily loss, max weekly loss — as specific numbers, not vague intentions.
- Review your Module 16 journal for any instance (even a near-miss) of FOMO, revenge trading, overtrading, or holding-on-hope, and write what specifically triggered it and what rule would have prevented it.

## 6. Drills

- **Max-loss-statement drill:** for 3 hypothetical position sizes, state the dollar and percentage max loss and whether it passes your own acceptability test.
- **Trigger-recognition drill:** given a hypothetical emotional scenario (e.g., "a token you passed on is up 300%"), identify which psychological trap it represents and the correct countermeasure.

## 7. Real-World Applications

- Your personal risk-control numbers from this module become the "Risk" sections of both Module 20 and Module 21 playbooks, and your psychology countermeasures become explicit checklist items alongside the Module 13 security checklist.

## 8. Challenges

- Recall (honestly) one moment from this course so far — even a paper/observational one — where you felt a pull toward FOMO, attachment, or overtrading, and write exactly what you did or would do to counter it.

## 9. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 65):** Which of the psychological traps in Section 3 do you suspect you're personally most vulnerable to, and why?

**Exit (Day 67):** Present your complete personal risk-control rule set (Section 5) with specific numbers, and correctly identify the right countermeasure for 4 out of 5 hypothetical psychological-trap scenarios.

## 10. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | No defined risk rules; unaware of own psychological patterns |
| Developing | Rules exist but are vague or inconsistently followed |
| Competent | Specific, written risk rules for both BTC/ETH and meme coins; can name own likely triggers |
| Advanced | Catches a real psychological trap in the moment (per journal evidence) and applies the countermeasure |
| Highly Proficient | Risk/psychology discipline holds even under a real losing streak or a real missed 10x |
| Mastery | Could coach someone else through a real FOMO/revenge-trading moment |

You need **Competent** to move to Module 18.
