# Module 15 — Meme-Coin Trading Framework

**Est. length:** 3 days (6 hours) to assemble and first-apply the framework. Refinement continues through Modules 16–18.
**Prerequisite:** Modules 9–14 (this module is the synthesis of everything before it). **Feeds into:** Module 16 (journaling/limited backtesting), Module 18 (paper trading it live), Module 21 (this becomes your permanent playbook).

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn

One primary, testable meme-coin trading framework — not ten strategies — that combines market condition (Module 9), discovery (Module 10), narrative/X vetting (Module 11), tokenomics (Module 12), security (Module 13), on-chain/chart reliability (Module 14), and risk management, into a single repeatable process with explicit discovery, qualification, research, entry criteria, confirmation, stop/invalidation, position sizing, target/exit, trade management, no-trade conditions, and risk controls.

## 2. Why It Matters

Every module so far has been a piece. Without assembling them into one disciplined sequence, you'll apply them inconsistently under real pressure (FOMO, a fast-moving chart) — exactly when discipline matters most. This framework is what turns "I know a lot about meme coins" into "I have a process I actually follow."

## 3. The Framework: Full Pipeline

### Stage 1 — Market Condition Check (Module 9)
Before any discovery session: what's the current cycle phase (accumulation/bull/distribution/bear), BTC dominance trend, and is there an active, strong narrative? **No-trade condition:** if the broad market condition is clearly risk-off (BTC dominance sharply rising, breadth collapsing), meme-coin conditions are structurally unfavorable — reduce activity or stand aside, regardless of how good an individual token looks.

### Stage 2 — Discovery (Module 10)
Source candidates via trending lists, new-launch monitoring, and narrative-shift tracking. Output: a list of candidates with basic data (age, market cap, liquidity, volume). **No decision made yet.**

### Stage 3 — Qualification (fast first-pass filter)
Before spending full research time, quickly reject candidates that fail any hard minimum, e.g.: liquidity below your personal minimum threshold, holder concentration obviously extreme at a glance, or an immediate honeypot/scanner red flag. This filter exists to protect your time, not replace full validation.

### Stage 4 — Research / Validation (Modules 11–14)
For every candidate that survives qualification, run in full:
- X/Twitter credibility and narrative check (Module 11)
- Tokenomics and wallet/holder analysis (Module 12)
- The Ultimate Meme-Coin Safety Checklist (Module 13)
- On-chain investigation and chart-reliability scoring (Module 14)

Output: a completed research writeup and a clear **qualify** or **reject** decision, with reasoning.

### Stage 5 — Entry Criteria
Only for qualified tokens. Define, in writing, before entering:
- What specific chart/on-chain condition triggers entry (e.g., a liquidity sweep + reclaim on a token that passed chart-reliability scoring as at least "moderate," or a confirmed narrative catalyst with fresh, broadening volume — pick the criteria that match your own tested observations, don't just copy an example)
- What **confirmation** you require beyond the trigger alone (e.g., sustained volume after the trigger, not just a single wick)

### Stage 6 — Stop/Invalidation
Define, before entry, the specific price level or condition that proves the thesis wrong (e.g., loss of a key structure level, or a security/on-chain red flag emerging post-entry such as a sudden insider sell). Both a **price-based** stop and a **condition-based** invalidation (e.g., "if LP gets pulled, exit immediately regardless of price," which may require manual/fast action rather than a standard stop order on some DEXs).

### Stage 7 — Position Sizing
Materially smaller than your BTC/ETH position sizing (Module 17 formalizes the numbers) — sized so that a full, fast loss (a realistic meme-coin outcome, not a tail case) is genuinely acceptable, not just "a shame."

### Stage 8 — Target / Exit Planning
Define your take-profit approach before entry: e.g., scaling out in tranches as price extends (given how fast meme coins can reverse, holding for a single large target with no partial exits is a materially different risk decision — make it deliberately, not by default).

### Stage 9 — Trade Management
Once in the trade: re-check the security/on-chain checklist periodically (things can change fast — an LP unlock, a sudden insider wallet movement), not just at entry.

### Stage 10 — No-Trade Conditions (explicit list)
- Market condition stage says risk-off (Stage 1)
- Token fails any hard item on the Module 13 safety checklist
- Chart-reliability score is "low" (Module 14) and you have no other corroborating validation
- You're trading from FOMO/urgency rather than a completed research writeup (Module 17 deepens this)
- You've already hit your daily/weekly risk limit (Module 17)

### Stage 11 — Risk Controls (Overview — Full Detail in Module 17)
Maximum position size per trade, maximum number of concurrent meme-coin positions, maximum daily/weekly loss limit specific to this specialization (separate from your BTC/ETH limits).

## 4. How to Test This Framework

Full backtesting (in the Module 7 BTC/ETH sense) is **not fully possible** for meme coins — market conditions, available tokens, and liquidity structures change too fast for a historical replay to represent future conditions reliably (Module 16 formalizes exactly what can and can't be tested). What *is* possible and required:
1. **Paper-trade the full framework** (Module 18) on real, live candidates for a meaningful sample before risking real capital.
2. **Journal every step** (Module 16) — not just entries/exits, but qualification/rejection decisions too, so you can later review whether your qualification filter itself is well-calibrated (rejecting genuine junk, not just being randomly restrictive or permissive).
3. **Review win rate, average R, and — critically — how often the no-trade conditions correctly kept you out of a loss** (a form of process-quality feedback distinct from win/loss stats alone).

## 5. Practical Exercises

- Write out your own personal version of Stages 5–10 in specific, concrete terms (not the generic examples above) — this becomes your first framework draft.
- Take one real qualified candidate from Module 12–14's work and run it through the complete framework, stage by stage, ending in either a defined paper-trade plan or a documented reject decision.

## 6. Drills

- **Stage-recall drill:** name all 11 stages from memory, in order, without notes.
- **No-trade-condition drill:** given a hypothetical scenario (e.g., "market condition is risk-off but the token looks amazing"), state the correct action per your framework.

## 7. Real-World Applications

- This framework, once refined through Modules 16–18, becomes the entire content of your Module 21 meme-coin playbook.

## 8. Challenges

- Walk through a real token, end-to-end, through all 11 stages, out loud or in writing, including at least one point where the framework would have stopped you from a trade you might otherwise have taken on impulse.

## 9. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 59):** Before building it, sketch what you currently imagine a "good meme-coin process" would look like, in your own words.

**Exit (Day 61):** Present your complete, personalized 11-stage framework in writing, and walk one real candidate through it end-to-end with a clear entry/reject decision and full reasoning at each stage.

## 10. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | No defined framework, decisions made ad hoc per token |
| Developing | Framework exists on paper, not consistently applied |
| Competent | Applies the full framework to a real candidate correctly and completely |
| Advanced | Catches own impulse-driven deviations from the framework in real time |
| Highly Proficient | Framework application becomes fast and automatic |
| Mastery | Could teach someone else to build and apply their own version of this framework |

You need **Competent** to move to Module 16.
