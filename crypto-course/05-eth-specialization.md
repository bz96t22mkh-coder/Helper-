## 1. What You Need to Learn

ETH market structure, ETH-specific liquidity/volatility, ETH's relationship with BTC (including the ETH/BTC ratio), ETH derivatives (funding, OI, liquidations), ETH's ecosystem role (staking, L2s, gas/burn), and ETH on-chain data.

## 2. Why It Matters

ETH is your second primary specialization, and it behaves differently from BTC in ways that matter for trading: it's more directly tied to on-chain economic activity (gas usage, L2 growth, DeFi activity), it has a variable/sometimes-deflationary supply mechanism, and it trades with a meaningful degree of independence from BTC that shows up as the ETH/BTC ratio — a genuinely useful, testable signal.

## 3. Foundational Concepts

- **ETH's supply is not fixed:** post-Merge, ETH issuance comes from staking rewards, partially offset by a fee-burn mechanism (EIP-1559) that destroys a portion of every transaction fee. Net effect on total supply varies with network activity — high on-chain activity can make ETH net-deflationary for periods; low activity can make it net-inflationary. This is fundamentally different from BTC's fixed schedule.
- **ETH as "the settlement layer for an economy":** ETH's value proposition ties to actual usage — DeFi, L2 activity, stablecoin settlement, tokenization — versus BTC's simpler store-of-value narrative. This means ETH-specific catalysts (a major protocol upgrade, L2 growth, stablecoin volume) can move ETH independently of BTC narrative.
- **ETH/BTC ratio:** the price of ETH denominated in BTC, not USD. A rising ratio = ETH outperforming BTC (often coincides with "risk-on," altcoin-favorable conditions); a falling ratio = ETH underperforming BTC (often coincides with BTC dominance rising). This ratio is one of the cleanest read-outs of relative crypto risk appetite.

## 4. Beginner Concepts

- **Staking:** ETH holders can lock ETH to help secure the network and earn yield. Large-scale staking/unstaking flows (especially after protocol changes that ease withdrawals) can be a meaningful supply-side factor to watch.
- **Layer 2s (Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, etc.):** most day-to-day cheap activity now happens here rather than on ETH mainnet, but L2s settle back to ETH for security — L2 growth is a fundamental tailwind for ETH's long-term thesis even when it reduces mainnet gas fee revenue short-term (a real, debated tension in ETH's narrative).
- **Gas/burn relationship to price action:** periods of high network usage (NFT mints, big DeFi activity, congestion) burn more ETH — informative context, not a standalone trading signal.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts

- **ETH's volatility vs. BTC's:** ETH has historically shown higher volatility than BTC on both the upside and downside — relevant for position sizing and stop placement even when using the same ICT framework.
- **Correlation with BTC, and where it breaks:** ETH and BTC are highly correlated most of the time (both are "majors"), but the ETH/BTC ratio trends over weeks/months as capital rotates preference between them — genuine divergence windows exist and are tradeable/relevant context, not noise to ignore.
- **ETH's derivatives market:** similar mechanics to BTC (funding, OI, liquidations) but often with different positioning extremes — ETH funding can run hotter (more speculative) than BTC's during altcoin-favorable regimes.

## 6. Advanced Concepts

- **Reading the ETH/BTC ratio as a regime filter:** a rising ratio alongside falling BTC dominance is corroborating evidence of a genuine risk-on/alt-season shift (Module 9); a rising ratio alone, without dominance confirmation, deserves more scrutiny before treating it as regime-confirming.
- **ETH on-chain data (introduced here, deepened in Module 8):** stablecoin supply growth on Ethereum/L2s (fresh capital entering DeFi), exchange netflows, and staking-queue changes are context layers, same probabilistic-not-predictive framing as BTC.
- **Narrative sensitivity:** ETH price action is more sensitive to ecosystem-specific news (upgrade dates, major protocol exploits, regulatory news about ETH specifically, e.g. ETF flows) than BTC is to equivalent BTC-specific news, simply because more of ETH's addressable narrative is about *usage* rather than *scarcity*.

## 8. Drills

- **ETH/BTC regime drill:** given a hypothetical ETH/BTC ratio direction plus a hypothetical BTC dominance direction, state whether they corroborate a risk-on or risk-off read, or conflict (and if they conflict, what that itself tells you — uncertainty, not a clean signal).
- **Volatility-adjustment drill:** given the same ICT setup type on BTC and ETH, explain how you'd think differently about stop distance/position size given ETH's typically higher volatility.
