# Section 12 — Decision-Making

**Length:** 7 days. **Goal:** choose well under real uncertainty — directly usable for trading, and everything else.

---

### Day 1 — Reversible vs. Irreversible

If a decision is easy to undo, decide fast and cheap — you can fix it later. If it's hard to reverse, it deserves real deliberation. Most people get this backwards: agonizing over what to eat, rushing what to commit long-term to.

**How to do it:** before spending time on any decision, ask "if this turns out wrong, how expensive and slow is it to undo?" Cheap and fast to undo → decide in minutes. Slow and expensive → actually deliberate.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: describe a real decision you're facing. How would you naturally approach it, no framework? |
| 20 | Classify 3 real upcoming decisions as reversible or irreversible. Note current time spent vs. what's appropriate. |
| 15 | Are you over-deliberating on reversible ones, or rushing irreversible ones? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — What You're Giving Up

Every choice means not doing the next-best alternative. Naming that alternative explicitly, instead of leaving it invisible, changes how attractive an option actually looks — "yes" to one thing is always also "no" to something else.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's classification. |
| 20 | For 2 real choices, name explicitly what you're giving up by choosing them. |
| 15 | For your baseline decision, list the gains and costs of each option side by side. |
| 20 | Did naming the opportunity cost change how attractive an option looked? |

### Day 3 — Size Times Likelihood

A small likely gain can beat a huge unlikely one, and vice versa. Rough out the possible outcomes, how likely each is, and how big — don't just ask "could this go well," ask "how often would this actually go well, and by how much."

**Worked example.** Option A: 90% chance of a small win. Option B: 10% chance of a huge win, 90% chance of nothing. Which is better depends entirely on the actual numbers, not on which one "feels" more exciting to imagine.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's trade-off table. |
| 25 | For 2 real uncertain choices, rough out possible outcomes, their likelihood, and their size. |
| 20 | Does this change which option looks best vs. your gut pick? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 4 — Mood vs. Facts

Fear, excitement, and impatience distort decisions without announcing themselves. **Loss aversion** overweights losses vs. equal gains (losing $100 feels worse than gaining $100 feels good). **Anchoring** overweights the first number you saw, even an arbitrary one. **Overconfidence** overrates your own certainty. Check for these explicitly, by name, before finalizing.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 25 | Take your baseline decision. Check it explicitly against loss aversion, anchoring, and overconfidence. |
| 20 | Which bias was most present, and how did checking for it shift your thinking? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Knowing When You Have Enough Information

Before gathering more data, ask: would this actually change my choice? If the answer is no, you already have enough — more research at that point is just stalling dressed up as diligence.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 25 | For your baseline decision, list what more info you're tempted to gather, then ask if it would actually change your choice. |
| 15 | Make 2 real small reversible decisions in under 60 seconds each. |
| 15 | Where do you typically stall gathering info that wouldn't change anything? |

### Day 6 — A Hard One, Full Process

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall this week's tools. |
| 40 | Take a real, harder, more irreversible decision on your plate. Run the full process: trade-offs, expected value, bias-check, information threshold, decide. |
| 15 | How does this decision feel now vs. before running the framework? |

### Day 7 — Exit Check

Take a new, genuinely undecided real decision. Classify it, map trade-offs, sketch expected value, check 2 biases, state your info-enough threshold, decide, and note what you'll review after.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 10 | Review a past decision — separate whether the process was good from whether it just got lucky. |
| 30 | The full exit task. |
| 15 | Reflection. |

**Mastery bar:** you triage by reversibility automatically and can name your own biases mid-decision. If not yet, repeat the full framework on a fresh real decision, focusing on expected value and bias-checking.
