# Section 12 — Decision-Making

**Est. length:** 6–10 sessions (core track: 7 days).
**Prerequisite:** Section 11 (Problem-Solving). **Feeds into:** Negotiation, Leadership, Strategic Thinking, Financial Literacy application.

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Decision frameworks, risk, uncertainty, expected value, opportunity cost, trade-offs, reversible vs. irreversible decisions, biases in decisions, emotional decision-making, information-gathering thresholds ("enough info"), and post-decision review.

## 2. Why It Matters
Problem-Solving generates good options; this section is choosing well between them under real uncertainty — directly transferable to trading decisions, life choices, and every later section that requires a real call (Negotiation, Leadership, Strategic Thinking).

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **Most decisions are made under uncertainty, not certainty:** the goal isn't finding the "guaranteed right" choice, it's making the best decision given what you can reasonably know now.
- **A good decision can have a bad outcome, and vice versa:** judge the decision by the quality of the process and information used at the time, not purely by how it turned out (this matters enormously for trading).
- **Not all decisions deserve equal deliberation:** the amount of time/effort should scale with the stakes and reversibility of the decision.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Reversible vs. irreversible ("one-way vs. two-way doors"):** reversible decisions should be made fast and cheaply (you can undo it); irreversible ones deserve real deliberation.
- **Opportunity cost:** every choice means not doing the next-best alternative — naming that alternative explicitly.
- **Basic trade-off mapping:** listing what you gain and give up with each option side by side.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Expected value (informal):** weighing an outcome by both its size and its likelihood, not just "could this go well" — a small likely gain can beat a huge unlikely one, and vice versa.
- **Emotional decision-making:** noticing when a decision is being driven by a current mood (fear, excitement, impatience) rather than the actual facts, and pausing to check.
- **Common decision biases:** loss aversion (overweighting losses vs. equivalent gains), anchoring (overrelying on the first number/option seen), overconfidence.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Information-gathering thresholds:** knowing when you have "enough" information to decide vs. when you're stalling (analysis paralysis) or gathering info that won't actually change the decision.
- **Structured decision frameworks:** using a simple weighted-criteria comparison for genuinely hard multi-option decisions.
- **Post-decision review:** after the outcome is known, separating "was this a good decision given what I knew" from "did it work out" — extracting the real lesson either way.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **Door-type sort:** for 3 real upcoming decisions, classify each as reversible or irreversible and adjust how much time you'll spend accordingly.
- **Opportunity-cost drill:** for a real choice, name explicitly what you're giving up by choosing it.
- **Expected-value sketch:** for a real uncertain choice, rough out the possible outcomes, their approximate likelihood, and their size.
- **Bias-check drill:** before finalizing a real decision, check it against loss aversion, anchoring, and overconfidence explicitly.

## 8. Drills
- **60-second reversible-decision drill:** practice making a genuinely reversible decision (what to eat, a small purchase) in under 60 seconds, deliberately, to build the "fast on reversible" muscle.
- **Information-enough drill:** for a real decision, write what additional information you're tempted to gather, then ask "would this actually change my choice?" — if not, decide now.
- **Post-decision review drill:** revisit a past decision (good or bad outcome) and separate process quality from outcome luck.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Apply reversible/irreversible triage to your actual week's decisions, including trading-related ones (position sizing, exits).
- Use expected-value thinking explicitly the next time you face a real uncertain choice with a clear up/downside.
- Run a post-decision review on a real past decision (course-related, financial, or personal).

## 10. Challenges
- Make a real, moderately significant reversible decision today in under 5 minutes, deliberately resisting the urge to over-deliberate.
- Take a real harder, more irreversible decision on your plate and run the full framework (trade-offs, expected value, bias-check, information threshold) before deciding.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Describe a real decision you're currently facing (any size). Walk me through how you'd naturally approach it right now, with no framework — what would you weigh, and how would you decide?

**Exit (Day 7):** Take a new real decision (ideally genuinely undecided). Classify it reversible/irreversible, map trade-offs and opportunity cost, sketch expected value, check for at least 2 biases, state your information-enough threshold, decide, and note what you'll review post-decision.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Decides purely on gut feeling or avoids deciding; no distinction between decision sizes |
| Developing | Can list trade-offs with prompting; still over- or under-deliberates regardless of stakes |
| Competent | Reliably triages by reversibility, maps trade-offs/opportunity cost, checks for obvious biases |
| Advanced | Uses expected value thinking and information thresholds effectively under real uncertainty |
| Highly Proficient | Decides efficiently and well even on complex, high-stakes, ambiguous decisions |
| Mastery | Judges own decisions by process quality, not outcome luck; continuously improves via post-decision review |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 13.
