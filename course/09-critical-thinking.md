# Section 9 — Critical Thinking

**Length:** 9 days. **Goal:** spot a bad argument — including your own — before you build a debate style on top of it.

---

### Day 1 — Claim, Reason, Evidence

Every argument has three parts: a claim, a reason for it, and evidence backing the reason. Most weak arguments are missing or hand-waving one of the three — breaking it down exposes exactly which.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: take a real argument you've seen recently (an article, an ad, a conversation). Break it into claim/reason/evidence, on instinct. |
| 20 | Break down 2 more real arguments the same way. |
| 15 | Where was the weakest link in each — reason or evidence? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — Hidden Assumptions

Most disagreements are actually about an assumption neither side has said out loud. Ask "what would have to be true for this claim to hold?" and you'll usually find it.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's breakdowns. |
| 25 | For 3 opinions you genuinely hold, write the unstated assumption each depends on. |
| 20 | Which assumption, if false, would most change your opinion? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 3 — Your Brain's Shortcuts

Confirmation bias makes you notice evidence that agrees with you and skip what doesn't. Availability bias makes a vivid recent example feel more common than it is. Sunk-cost makes you keep going just because you already invested. These aren't stupidity — they're default settings everyone runs on unless they check for them.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 20 | Identify 2 real instances (yours or someone else's) of these biases from the last week. |
| 20 | Which bias do you personally fall into most often? |
| 15 | Reflection. |

### Day 4 — Common Fallacies

Straw man attacks a distorted version of the argument. Ad hominem attacks the person instead of the claim. False dichotomy pretends there are only two options. Slippery slope assumes one step guarantees an extreme outcome. Appeal to authority treats "an expert said so" as proof on its own. Once you can name these, you start seeing them everywhere — including in your own arguments.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 25 | Find and identify 3 fallacies in real content — ads, social media, a remembered argument. |
| 20 | For one, write what a valid, non-fallacious version of that argument would look like. |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Checkpoint

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Take a new real argument. Identify claim/reason/evidence, a hidden assumption, and any fallacy present. |
| 15 | Rep on weakest element. |
| 10 | Progress vs. Day 1. |

### Day 6 — Steelmanning

Before responding to a view you disagree with, state it in its strongest, most charitable form — often stronger than its actual proponents state it. If your response still holds against the strongest version, it's a real response.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 20 | Before defending 2 of your own opinions, write the strongest objection to each first. |
| 20 | Pick a position you disagree with. Write its strongest possible version. |
| 15 | Did steelmanning change your confidence in your own opposing view? |

### Day 7 — How Good Is This Evidence, Really

A stat, a single story, and a large representative sample are not the same weight of evidence. Check source quality, sample size, and whether it's correlation being sold as causation.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Take 3 pieces of "evidence" you've encountered recently. Rate their actual quality and representativeness. |
| 15 | Which piece did you previously trust more than it deserved? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 8 — Thinking in Probabilities

"Certainly true" and "certainly false" are rarely honest. Attach a rough confidence — "I'm about 70% sure" — and update it as evidence comes in.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 25 | State 5 factual beliefs with a probability attached, then check each against a real source. |
| 20 | Where were you most overconfident, and in which direction? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 9 — Exit Check

Steelman both sides of a real debate topic, identify a fallacy or weak assumption on each side, state your own position with a confidence level and what would change your mind. Send it to me.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 40 | The full exit task, in writing or recorded. |
| 15 | Compare to Day 1 — what do you notice now that you didn't then? |

**Mastery bar:** you catch fallacies and hidden assumptions in real time, including your own, and can steelman a view you disagree with fairly. If not yet, repeat fallacy-spotting and steelmanning on fresh material.
