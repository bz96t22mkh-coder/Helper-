# Module 3 — Making Gaming + Reaction Work Together

## 1. Concept

Two content categories under one brand don't feel random when they share a **throughline** — the same personality, humor style, and POV showing up regardless of format. What ties Gaming and Reaction together isn't the subject matter (a game vs. a viral clip) — it's *you reacting to and commentating on something in real time*. Reframe it: you're not running a "gaming channel" and a "reaction channel." You're running a **commentary/reaction channel where the source material sometimes is a game and sometimes is a clip.** That single reframe is what makes the combination coherent instead of scattered.

## 2. Why it matters

Channels that feel random (this week: gameplay, next week: a totally unrelated reaction, next week: a vlog) train the algorithm and the viewer that they don't know what they're getting — this hurts suggested/browse placement (unclear audience signal) and hurts subscriber retention (people subscribed for X, got Y, tune out). Defining clear content pillars fixes both problems at once.

## 3. Content pillars — the framework

### Core pillar(s) — 60–70% of uploads
The 1–2 formats that are unambiguously "what this channel is." These should be the ones that best showcase your specific personality strength from Module 2. Pick based on where your natural energy is strongest, not what's most trendy this month.

### Secondary pillar(s) — 20–30% of uploads
Formats that support and vary the core without confusing it. A gaming-core channel might have "reacting to gaming news/trailers/clips" as secondary — same audience, same personality, different source material, gives you upload flexibility when there's no game content ready.

### Experimental slot — 10% or less
New formats you're testing. Kept small and clearly labeled (in your own head, not necessarily to viewers) so a flop doesn't feel like "the channel is falling apart" — it's just a test.

## 4. How Gaming and Reaction complement each other through personality

- **Gaming videos build your "extended" persona** — how you behave under pressure/frustration/fear, your problem-solving style, your running commentary during long-form focus. This is where deep personality texture shows.
- **Reaction videos build your "rapid-take" persona** — how you process something fast, your hot takes, your comedic timing on shorter material. This is where your wit and quick opinions shine.
- Together, a viewer gets a *fuller* picture of who you are than either format alone would give — which is exactly the personality-first outcome you're going for. Cross-reference: a gaming video can include a reaction beat (reacting to a clip/trailer related to the game you're playing); a reaction video can include gaming-literate commentary that only someone who plays games would make. This is the actual "working together" — not two separate lanes, but ingredients you mix within videos, not just across your upload calendar.

## 5. Determining core vs. secondary vs. experimental

Ask three questions for any format:
1. **Does it showcase my strongest personality trait from Module 2?** If yes → core candidate.
2. **Can I produce it consistently without burning out?** (Reaction content is generally faster to produce than long gameplay sessions — factor that into your cadence, not just your preference.)
3. **Does my early data (views, retention, comments) support it?** You won't know this until you have some videos up — that's fine, revisit this framework after your first 15–20 videos (see Module 21).

## 6. Recurring series — why they matter here specifically

A recurring series (same format, same title structure, recognizable intro/segment, recurring rating system, etc.) does double duty: it reinforces brand identity (Module 2) AND gives viewers a reason to return specifically for *that thing*, not just "your channel" abstractly. Examples of series shapes to adapt: a weekly "reacting to the internet's worst gaming takes," a running horror-game series with a consistent fear/bravery rating you give yourself at the end, a recurring "guess what happens next" reaction format.

## 7. Examples

- Core: long-form horror gameplay with heavy commentary. Secondary: reacting to horror-game trailers/community clips. Experimental: occasional non-gaming reaction to something viral and topical. All three share the same "genuinely gets scared, jokes about it immediately after" personality throughline.
- Core: comedic multiplayer gameplay with a friend group. Secondary: reacting to clips from the same games/community. Experimental: IRL-adjacent reaction content. Throughline: fast, chaotic banter-driven humor across all three.

## 8. Exercises

1. List every video format you're excited to make. Sort each into Core / Secondary / Experimental using the 3 questions in Section 5.
2. For your top 2 core formats, write the one-sentence throughline that would make a viewer say "oh yeah, that's so [you]" regardless of which one they're watching.
3. Sketch one recurring series concept with a name, a repeatable structure, and a recurring element (rating, segment, bit).

## 10. Checklist

- [ ] Core pillars chosen based on personality strength + production sustainability, not just excitement
- [ ] At least one secondary pillar identified for calendar flexibility
- [ ] Throughline sentence written and it's about personality/POV, not subject matter
- [ ] At least one recurring series sketched with a name and repeatable structure

## 11. Common mistakes

- Treating "Gaming" and "Reaction" as two separate channels sharing a login instead of one brand with two source-material types.
- Picking core pillars purely by what's trending instead of what showcases your personality and is sustainable to produce weekly.
- Never developing a recurring series — every video starts from zero brand recognition instead of compounding.

## 12. What beginners waste time on

- Endlessly debating the "perfect" pillar split before posting anything. Pick a reasonable split, post, and let Module 17 (Analytics) and Module 21 (First 100 Videos) tell you what to adjust.
- Trying to keep gaming and reaction content in totally separate "modes" instead of letting them cross-pollinate within videos.

## 14. Checkpoint

You should be able to state, in one breath: your core pillar(s), your secondary pillar(s), and the personality throughline that connects them. If your throughline is a topic ("gaming") instead of a personality trait ("overthinking everything out loud"), redo Section 6 of Module 2 and Section 4 here before advancing.
