# Section 1 — Meta-Learning

**Est. length:** 4–7 sessions (core track: 6 days). Extend only if the Day 6 exit assessment shows gaps.
**Prerequisite:** none. **Feeds into:** every later section's review segments; your day-trading course; independent learning generally.

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Active recall, spaced repetition, deliberate practice, interleaving, the Feynman technique, memory basics, concentration/focus, note-taking that supports recall (not transcription), understanding vs. memorization, mental models, identifying your own knowledge gaps, self-testing, how to tackle a genuinely difficult subject, why passive learning (re-reading, highlighting, watching) fails, how to design a study session, and how to learn something with no teacher.

## 2. Why It Matters
You are about to run this 20-section course, a day-trading course, and a YouTube course at the same time, for months. The difference between that going well and going nowhere is almost entirely *how* you study, not how much willpower you have. Get this right first and every other section (plus your other courses) gets faster and stickier.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **Passive vs. active learning:** re-reading/highlighting/watching creates a false sense of fluency (it *feels* like learning because the material is familiar) without building retrieval strength. Only effortful recall builds it.
- **The forgetting curve:** you lose most new information within days unless you retrieve it again at increasing intervals — this is *why* spaced repetition works, not just a study trick.
- **Desirable difficulty:** learning that feels a bit effortful and error-prone (recall, testing, spacing) produces more durable knowledge than learning that feels smooth (re-reading).

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Active recall:** closing the source and trying to reproduce the information from memory (not "did I recognize this," but "can I generate this").
- **Spaced repetition:** reviewing the same material at expanding intervals (e.g., 1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 16 days) right before you'd otherwise forget it.
- **Self-testing:** turning any material into a question you must answer without looking, before you feel "ready."

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **The Feynman technique:** explain a concept in plain language as if teaching a smart 12-year-old; every place you stumble, hedge, or reach for jargon marks a real gap — go fix that gap, then re-explain.
- **Deliberate practice:** practice aimed at your specific current weak point, with a clear target and immediate feedback — not just "doing the activity a lot."
- **Interleaving:** mixing multiple related topics/skills within one session instead of blocking all your time on one thing — harder in the moment, much better for long-term retention and transfer.
- **Note-taking for recall:** notes as question-prompts and summarized-in-your-own-words maps, not verbatim transcription.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Mental models:** reusable frameworks (e.g., opportunity cost, second-order effects, supply/demand) that let you reason about *new* situations instead of memorizing one-off facts.
- **Identifying knowledge gaps before you're tested on them:** deliberately trying to explain or apply a topic *before* you feel confident, specifically to surface what you don't actually know.
- **Designing a full study session:** sequencing retrieval-of-old-material → new material → interleaved practice → self-test, inside a fixed time box, for any subject.
- **Learning independently:** when there's no teacher/curriculum (e.g., your own day-trading material), building your own retrieval questions and spacing schedule from raw source material.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **Blank-page recall:** close the book/video, write everything you remember about yesterday's material for 3 minutes, then check what you missed.
- **Feynman pass:** explain a concept out loud in one unbroken take, flag every stumble, look up only the stumbles, re-explain.
- **Session design:** given a topic and 45 minutes, write out your own session plan (retrieval / new material / interleave / test) before executing it.
- **Gap-hunting:** before starting a new lesson (in any course), predict 3 questions you think it will answer — this exposes what you don't yet know to look for.

## 8. Drills
- **60-second recall drill:** pick any concept learned in the last week, recall-dump it in 60 seconds, score yourself 1–5 on completeness.
- **Question-generation drill:** turn a page of notes/video into 5 test-yourself questions in under 3 minutes.
- **Interleave drill:** alternate 5-minute blocks between two unrelated topics (e.g., a trading concept and an English drill) and notice how it changes your recall vs. blocking them.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Apply the full loop to your **day-trading course**: don't rewatch lessons — build recall questions from your notes and test yourself before each new lesson.
- Apply it to **this course itself**: each section's daily "review" segment is spaced retrieval of yesterday's/last week's material, by design.
- Apply it to **any book you're reading**: after each chapter, close it and do a 90-second recall dump before moving on.

## 10. Challenges
- Take a topic from your day-trading course you found confusing. Use only active recall + Feynman (no re-reading the source) to get to a clean, jargon-free explanation within 20 minutes.
- Design and run a full 30-minute independent study session, from scratch, on a topic with no existing course structure (e.g., a work skill or a hobby).

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1, before instruction):** Pick a concept from your day-trading course (or any course) you haven't reviewed in the last week. Without looking at notes, speak/write your explanation of it for 90 seconds. Then check it against the source and mark what was missing, vague, or wrong. Send me: what you recalled, and where you got stuck.

**Exit (Day 6):** Design a 30-minute independent study session for a topic you haven't formally studied yet (in day trading or elsewhere) using retrieval + interleaving + self-testing, execute it in real time, then teach the concept back to me in under 2 minutes with zero notes. I evaluate the session design *and* the final explanation.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Still defaults to re-reading/re-watching; can't design a session unprompted |
| Developing | Uses active recall when reminded; session design needs heavy scaffolding |
| Competent | Reliably self-tests and spaces review without prompting; can design a basic session |
| Advanced | Designs full interleaved sessions independently; catches own knowledge gaps before being tested |
| Highly Proficient | Applies this automatically across every course/subject without thinking about it |
| Mastery | Could teach someone else how to learn a subject from scratch |

You need **Competent** on the exit assessment to move to Section 2. (Mastery is expected to keep developing through use, not by Day 6.)

## 13. Day-by-Day Training Plan

### Day 1 — Foundations: Active Recall & the Forgetting Curve (60 min)
| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline assessment | 15 | Run the baseline task above (Section 11). Send me your recall + self-check notes. |
| Learn | 10 | Read Section 3–4 above (passive vs. active, forgetting curve, active recall, spaced repetition). |
| Exercise: Blank-page recall | 15 | Pick a *different* concept from any course. Study it fresh for 5 min, then close it and recall-dump for 3 min, twice, comparing improvement. |
| Apply | 10 | Pick one real topic from your day-trading course. Turn today's or last week's material into 3 recall questions (no answers written yet). |
| Reflection | 10 | Write 3–4 sentences: what surprised you about how little you actually remembered without looking? |

### Day 2 — Feynman Technique + Finding Gaps (60 min)
| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Recall-test yourself on yesterday's 3 questions from memory before checking answers. |

| Exercise: Gap-fix | 15 | Look up *only* the stumble points, then re-explain the whole concept clean, in under 90 seconds. |
| Reflection | 10 | What did the stumbles reveal that you *thought* you understood but didn't? |

### Day 3 — Deliberate Practice + Interleaving (60 min)
| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|

### Day 5 — Full Independent Application (60 min)
| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Recall-test Day 4's mental model examples. |
| Apply (real session) | 35 | Actually run the session you designed on Day 4, on real material (day-trading course ideal), using the full loop: retrieval, new material, interleave if relevant, self-test at the end. |

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|

| Evaluation | — | I score you against the mastery table and tell you: repeat, extend, or progress to Section 2. |

**If exit assessment lands below Competent:** Days 7–8 repeat the Day 4–5 loop on a new topic with tighter feedback on the specific weak step, then reassess. **If Competent+:** move to Section 2, and these skills now get 2–5 minute maintenance reps at the start of future sections.
