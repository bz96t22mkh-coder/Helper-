# Section 1 — Meta-Learning

**Length:** 6 days. **Goal:** learn how to learn anything faster, then use it immediately on your other courses (day trading included).

---

### Day 1 — Active Recall

Your brain confuses familiarity with knowledge. Re-reading notes or re-watching a video feels like learning because the material feels familiar — but familiarity isn't the same as being able to produce the information from nothing. The only real test is closing the source and trying to explain it cold.

**How to do it, step by step:**
1. Pick something you learned recently.
2. Close your notes completely — don't have them open "just in case."
3. Set a timer for 90 seconds and explain it out loud or in writing, as if to someone who's never heard of it.
4. Every time you stumble or go blank, don't stop to look it up — just note the gap ("I don't actually know how X connects to Y") and keep going to the end of the 90 seconds.
5. Only now do you check the source, and only for the specific gaps you found.

**Worked example.** Say you're trying to recall "support and resistance" from trading. Weak version: you re-read the definition, nod, feel like you get it, move on. Active recall version: you close the notes and say out loud, "support is a price level where... it tends to stop falling because... [gap — you're not sure why]. Resistance is the opposite, where it stops rising because [gap]." Those two gaps are now a precise, searchable question instead of a vague sense of "I should probably review this again sometime."

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: pick a concept from your day-trading course you haven't reviewed in a week. 90 seconds, no notes, explain it. Check what you got wrong. |
| 15 | Do the same drill on a second concept. |
| 15 | Turn today's material (from any course) into 3 questions you'll test yourself on tomorrow, without writing the answers. |
| 15 | Try to answer those 3 questions right now, cold, before you've "studied" them at all. See what you already know. |

### Day 2 — The Feynman Technique

If you can't explain something in plain language, you don't understand it — you've memorized the shape of it. The fix: explain it as if to a smart 12-year-old, using no jargon. The moment you reach for a technical term or your explanation gets hand-wavy, you've found the actual edge of your understanding.

**How to do it:**
1. Pick a concept.
2. Explain it out loud in one continuous go — plain language only, no jargon allowed.
3. Every time you catch yourself using a term you couldn't define, or your explanation gets vague ("and then basically it just... does the thing"), mark it.
4. Look up *only* those marked spots.
5. Re-explain the whole thing clean, from the top.

**Worked example.** Explaining "compound interest" to a beginner: "If you have money in an account and it earns interest, next year you earn interest not just on your original money, but on the interest you already earned too — so it grows a little faster each year instead of by the same flat amount." No jargon, no formulas, and if you can't say that last clause confidently, you've found your gap: you understand *that* it compounds but not *why* the growth accelerates.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 10 | Retest yesterday's 3 questions from memory. |
| 20 | Feynman pass on a trading concept: explain it plain-language, no jargon, mark every stumble. |
| 20 | Look up only the stumbles, then re-explain the whole concept clean in under 90 seconds. |
| 10 | Same pass on one concept from this course. |

### Day 3 — Deliberate Practice & Interleaving

Practicing something 100 times doesn't make you better if you're not targeting your actual weak point — that's just repetition. **Deliberate practice** means: identify the one specific thing you got wrong, isolate it, and drill exactly that with immediate feedback (check yourself right after each rep, don't wait until the end).

**Interleaving** means mixing two different topics in one session instead of blocking all your time on one. It feels harder in the moment — you have to re-identify which approach applies each time instead of running the same one on autopilot — but that extra effort is exactly what makes the memory stick. Blocked practice (20 minutes of just A) feels smoother while you're doing it and is remembered worse a week later; interleaved practice (5 min A, 5 min B, repeat) feels harder in the moment and is remembered better.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Identify your single weakest point from Days 1–2. Do 3 targeted reps on just that, checking yourself immediately after each one. |
| 25 | Interleave: 5 min topic A, 5 min topic B, 5 min topic A, 5 min topic B, 5 min topic A. |
| 20 | Test yourself on both topics now. Compare: did interleaving feel harder in the moment, and did it actually stick better? |

### Day 4 — Mental Models & Designing a Study Session

A mental model is a reusable lens — like "opportunity cost" (what am I giving up by choosing this) or "second-order effects" (and then what happens after that) — that lets you reason about a brand-new situation instead of memorizing a fact that only applies once. You collect maybe 20–30 of these over time, and most new situations turn out to be a combination of ones you already have.

**A study session has a fixed shape, always in this order:**
1. Retrieve (5 min) — test yourself cold on what you covered last time, before looking at anything new.
2. New material (15 min) — take in the new content.
3. Interleave (15 min) — mix the new material with something else you're also learning.
4. Test (10 min) — self-test on everything from this session.

That shape works for anything — a trading concept, a language, this course. You're not "studying," you're running this specific four-step loop.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Take one mental model (e.g. opportunity cost). Apply it to explain two unrelated real situations — one from trading, one from daily life. |
| 30 | Design a 30-minute study session using the exact four-step shape above, for something you'll actually study this week. |
| 15 | Reflect: what would you have done before today instead — just re-read? |

|---|---|

**Mastery bar:** you self-test without being told to, and you can design a session for a brand-new topic without a template in front of you. If you're not there, we repeat Days 4–5 on new material — not the whole section.
