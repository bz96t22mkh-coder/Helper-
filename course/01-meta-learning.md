# Section 1 — Meta-Learning

**Length:** 6 days. **Goal:** learn how to learn anything faster, then use it immediately on your other courses (day trading included).

---

### Day 1 — Active Recall

Your brain confuses familiarity with knowledge. Re-reading notes or re-watching a video feels like learning because the material feels familiar — but familiarity isn't the same as being able to produce the information from nothing. The only real test is closing the source and trying to explain it cold.

**How to do it:** pick something you learned recently. Close your notes. Set a timer for 90 seconds and explain it out loud or in writing, as if to someone who knows nothing about it. Every time you stumble or go blank, that's a real gap you didn't know you had — note it, but don't stop to look it up yet. When the 90 seconds are up, check your notes only for the specific gaps.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: pick a concept from your day-trading course you haven't reviewed in a week. 90 seconds, no notes, explain it. Check what you got wrong. |
| 15 | Do the same drill on a second concept. |
| 15 | Turn today's material (from any course) into 3 questions you'll test yourself on tomorrow, without writing the answers. |
| 15 | Try to answer those 3 questions right now, cold, before you've "studied" them at all. See what you already know. |

### Day 2 — The Feynman Technique

If you can't explain something in plain language, you don't understand it — you've memorized the shape of it. The fix: explain it as if to a smart 12-year-old, using no jargon. The moment you reach for a technical term or your explanation gets hand-wavy, you've found the actual edge of your understanding.

**How to do it:** pick a concept. Explain it out loud in one go, plain language only. Mark every stumble. Go look up *only* those stumbles. Re-explain the whole thing clean.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 10 | Retest yesterday's 3 questions from memory. |
| 20 | Feynman pass on a trading concept: explain it plain-language, no jargon, mark every stumble. |
| 20 | Look up only the stumbles, then re-explain the whole concept clean in under 90 seconds. |
| 10 | Same pass on one concept from this course. |

### Day 3 — Deliberate Practice & Interleaving

Practicing something 100 times doesn't make you better if you're not targeting your actual weak point — that's just repetition. Deliberate practice means picking the *one* thing you got wrong last time and drilling exactly that, with immediate feedback. Interleaving means mixing two different topics in one session instead of blocking all your time on one — it feels harder in the moment but builds recall that actually holds, because your brain has to identify which approach to use each time instead of just repeating the last one.

**How to do it:** take your weakest point from Day 1 or 2, and do 3 focused reps on that specific thing. Then alternate between two different topics in 5-minute blocks instead of doing 20 minutes of one.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Identify your single weakest point from Days 1–2. Do 3 targeted reps on just that. |
| 25 | Interleave: 5 min topic A, 5 min topic B, 5 min topic A, 5 min topic B, 5 min topic A. |
| 20 | Compare: did interleaving feel harder than blocking one topic? Test yourself on both — which one actually stuck? |

### Day 4 — Mental Models & Designing a Study Session

A mental model is a reusable lens — like "opportunity cost" or "second-order effects" — that lets you reason about a brand-new situation instead of memorizing a fact that only applies once. Once you have a few, you stop needing to be taught every specific case.

A good study session has a fixed shape: retrieve what you learned last time (5 min) → take in new material (15 min) → interleave it with something else (15 min) → test yourself on all of it (10 min). That shape works for anything — a trading concept, a language, this course.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Take one mental model (e.g. opportunity cost). Apply it to explain two unrelated real situations. |
| 30 | Design a 30-minute study session (retrieve → new → interleave → test) for something you'll actually study this week. |
| 15 | Reflect: what would you have done before today instead — just re-read? |

### Day 5 — Run It For Real

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Retest Day 4's mental-model examples from memory. |
| 35 | Run the session you designed on Day 4, on real material — retrieve, new material, interleave, self-test. |
| 10 | Write 5 test questions from today's material for next week. |
| 10 | Rate how "active" this felt (1–5) vs. your old study habits. |

### Day 6 — Exit Check

Design and run a 30-minute session on a brand-new topic, from scratch, using the full loop. Then teach the concept back in under 2 minutes with zero notes. Send me both — the session design and the explanation — and I'll tell you if you're ready to move on or need another round.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 40 | Design + run the session, then explain the topic back cold, no notes. |
| 20 | Self-rate first, then compare against my evaluation. |

**Mastery bar to move on:** you self-test without being told to, and you can design a session for a brand-new topic without a template in front of you. If you're not there, we repeat Days 4–5 on new material — not the whole section.
