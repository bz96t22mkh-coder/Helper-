# Section 13 — Negotiation

**Est. length:** 10–16 sessions (core track: 12 days).
**Prerequisite:** Section 4 (Communication), Section 5 (EQ), Section 12 (Decision-Making). **Feeds into:** Leadership (internal negotiation), Networking (asks/favors).

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Preparation, BATNA, leverage, anchoring, making offers, handling concessions, asking questions, listening, persuasion, win-win framing, negotiating with difficult personalities, and both everyday and professional negotiation.

## 2. Why It Matters
This draws directly on communication, EQ, and decision-making frameworks you already have — negotiation is where they combine into getting real, concrete outcomes in situations with competing interests, from a price to a deadline to a disagreement about who does what.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **Preparation wins more than tactics:** knowing your BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement), your actual priorities, and the other side's likely priorities before you start matters more than any clever line mid-negotiation.
- **Negotiation is not adversarial by default:** the best outcomes usually come from finding where interests overlap (win-win), not from grinding the other side down.
- **Leverage is situational, not fixed:** your leverage depends on your BATNA, urgency, and information — and it can shift during the negotiation.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **BATNA:** identifying your real fallback if this negotiation fails — this sets your walk-away point and your actual confidence.
- **Basic preparation:** knowing your target, your walk-away point, and at least one guess at the other side's priorities before you start.
- **Making a clear offer:** stating a specific, concrete ask rather than a vague hint.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Anchoring:** the first number/offer disproportionately shapes the whole negotiation — understanding when to anchor first vs. let the other side anchor.
- **Concessions:** giving something up deliberately and signaling it has value (not giving in easily), and asking for something in return.
- **Questions and listening in negotiation:** using open questions to surface the other side's real priorities (which are often not what they first state) before making moves.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Win-win framing:** finding and proposing trades where both sides get more of what they actually care about, by surfacing priorities that aren't purely competitive (e.g., trading on timeline vs. price if one side cares more about one than the other).
- **Persuasion in negotiation:** applying rhetoric (Section 10) — credible framing, genuine stakes, sound logic — to make your position land, not just state it.
- **Difficult personalities:** staying regulated (Section 5) and holding your position calmly against aggressive, evasive, or manipulative tactics without mirroring them.
- **Everyday vs. professional negotiation:** the same core skills applied to a price negotiation, a deadline, a chore-split, or a salary/contract conversation — recognizing it's the same underlying skill in different clothes.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **BATNA-mapping drill:** for a real upcoming negotiation, write your actual BATNA and walk-away point before anything else.
- **Anchor drill:** practice opening with a deliberate, justified anchor in a low-stakes negotiation.
- **Priority-surfacing drill:** in a negotiation roleplay, ask 3 open questions before making any offer, to find out what the other side actually cares about.
- **Win-win trade drill:** given two parties' stated priorities, find a trade that gives both more of what they care about most.

## 8. Drills
- **Concession drill:** practice conceding something small deliberately, explicitly signaling its value, and asking for something back.
- **Difficult-personality drill:** roleplay (written or spoken, with yourself playing both sides if needed) a negotiation against an aggressive or evasive counterpart, practicing calm, non-mirrored responses.
- **Everyday-negotiation drill:** negotiate one small real everyday thing this week (a price, a deadline, a favor) using the prepared BATNA + question-first approach.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Prepare BATNA and priorities before your next real negotiation, however small.
- Use open questions before offers in your next real negotiation to find the other side's actual priority.
- Apply win-win framing the next time you're negotiating with someone you'll deal with again (most negotiations in real life are repeated-relationship, not one-shot).

## 10. Challenges
- Run one real negotiation this week (price, deadline, terms, a household/relationship negotiation) using the full prepared process.
- Handle one real difficult-personality negotiation moment (real or a realistic roleplay) while staying calm and non-defensive.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Recall your last real negotiation (however small — a price, a chore split, a deadline). How did you approach it, and what was the outcome? Did you know your BATNA at the time?

**Exit (Day 12):** Real-world task — run one real negotiation (or a realistic detailed roleplay if none is available this week) using: a prepared BATNA and walk-away point, at least 2 open questions before offering, a deliberate anchor or counter-anchor, at least one signaled concession with something asked in return, and a win-win framing attempt. Report the full process and outcome.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Negotiates reactively with no prep; accepts or rejects without exploring trades |
| Developing | Has a target price/ask but no real BATNA or priority-surfacing |
| Competent | Prepares BATNA, asks questions before offering, makes deliberate offers/concessions |
| Advanced | Finds win-win trades; handles anchoring and difficult personalities calmly |
| Highly Proficient | Negotiates well across everyday and professional contexts, reads leverage shifts live |
| Mastery | Negotiation feels collaborative and low-stress even in high-stakes situations |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 14.
