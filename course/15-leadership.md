# Section 15 — Leadership

**Length:** 12 days. **Goal:** get people moving in a good direction through trust and clarity — applies whether or not you have a title.

---

### Day 1 — Own the Outcome, Not the Excuse

A leader owns what happened, including the parts that went wrong, instead of explaining it away. That's what actually earns trust — not being right all the time.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: describe a real time you were in a leadership position, formal or not. What did you own, and where did you dodge? |
| 20 | Take a real recent setback and rewrite your account of it in full-ownership language. |
| 15 | What did you want to blame externally that's actually yours to own? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — Saying the Direction Clearly

A vague direction gets a vague result. State the expectation in one unambiguous sentence instead of hinting at it.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's reframe. |
| 30 | State a clear direction or expectation for a real, even informal, group situation this week, in one unambiguous sentence. |
| 15 | Was your normal instinct vague or hedged? What changed when you forced clarity? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 3 — Handing Off the Outcome, Not the Method

Delegate with a clear outcome and enough context — not a script for exactly how to do it. Micromanaging the method defeats the point of delegating at all.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Hand off a real task, even a small one, with a clear outcome stated and no instructions on method. |
| 15 | Did you resist letting go of the "how"? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 4 — Catching Gaps Early

Address a small accountability gap directly and early, before it grows — not after it's already a problem.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Address one small real accountability gap directly this week, yours or someone else's, early. |
| 15 | How did addressing it early feel compared to your normal tendency to let it slide? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Checkpoint

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall this week's toolkit. |
| 30 | Set clear direction for a real group situation, and delegate one real task with an outcome, not instructions. |
| 15 | Rep on weakest element. |
| 10 | Progress vs. Day 1. |

### Day 6 — Explaining the Why, Not Just the What

When a decision affects others, communicate the reasoning behind it, not just the decision itself — it's what people actually remember and trust.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Make a real decision that affects others. Communicate not just the decision but the reasoning behind it. |
| 15 | Did explaining the reasoning change how it was received, or how confident you felt making it? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 7 — Addressing Tension Directly

Avoiding a real tension doesn't make it go away — it just delays it and lets it grow. Address it directly and early, using regulation and negotiation skills, not avoidance.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Address a real tension, between others or with you, directly this week. |
| 15 | What was your instinct — avoid, or address? What made it easier or harder than expected? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 8 — Different People Want Different Things

Recognition, autonomy, mastery, or purpose — what motivates one person doesn't motivate another. Read what actually drives the specific person in front of you.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | For 2–3 people you interact with, guess what actually motivates each, based on real observed behavior. Adjust one real ask accordingly. |
| 15 | Were you using a one-size-fits-all approach with people who need different things? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 9 — Feedback That's Actually Useful

Specific and behavior-focused lands; vague or personality-focused doesn't. Give positive feedback as deliberately as corrective.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Write and deliver one specific positive and one specific corrective piece of feedback for real situations. |
| 15 | How specific was your feedback compared to what you'd normally say? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 10 — Checkpoint

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall the full toolkit. |
| 30 | Navigate a real decision affecting others, communicate the reasoning, and give one piece of specific feedback. |
| 25 | Progress vs. Day 5. |

### Day 11 — Real Autonomy

Trust matters most when it's tested — do your actions actually match your commitments? And can you give real autonomy instead of checking in constantly?

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Review your last week — did actions match stated commitments? Where didn't they? |
| 35 | Lead a real, even informal, group task this week using direction-setting, delegation, and follow-through — giving real autonomy, not constant check-ins. |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 12 — Exit Check

Give the full account of leading a real group effort: direction-setting, delegation, feedback, and full ownership of any setback. Send it to me.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall the full toolkit. |
| 40 | The full exit task. |
| 15 | Compare to Day 1's baseline — what's structurally different in how you'd lead that same situation now? |

**Mastery bar:** you set clear direction, delegate with outcomes not instructions, and own setbacks — as a default. If not yet, repeat delegation, feedback, and direct-conflict drills in fresh real situations.
