# Section 10 — Debating

**Est. length:** 8–14 sessions (core track: 10 days).
**Prerequisite:** Section 9 (Critical Thinking), Section 3 (Articulation). **Feeds into:** Negotiation (structured disagreement), Leadership (defending decisions).

**Framing:** trains accurate reasoning and clear, persuasive delivery — not "winning at all costs." You argue both sides throughout, on purpose.

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Building arguments live, rebuttals, rhetoric, structured disagreement, arguing positions you don't personally hold, and persuasive delivery while under pushback.

## 2. Why It Matters
This is critical thinking (Section 9) plus articulation (Section 3) plus confidence (Section 7), pressure-tested out loud. Every prerequisite is now in place — this section is where they get combined and stress-tested.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **A debate argument needs a full spine:** Claim → Reason → Evidence → Conclusion, delivered as one continuous unit, not a claim followed by a vague gesture at support.
- **Disagreement can be structured, not personal:** attacking the argument, not the person, and staying curious about *why* someone holds a view, keeps a debate productive instead of hostile.
- **Rhetoric is not manipulation:** ethos (credibility), pathos (emotional resonance), logos (logic) are the three real levers of persuasion — using them honestly (accurate credibility, genuine emotional stakes, sound logic) is the goal, not tricking anyone.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Claim-Reason-Evidence-Conclusion (CREC) delivery:** saying all four parts out loud, cleanly, in one go, on a topic given with only brief prep.
- **Basic rebuttal:** directly addressing the *strongest* part of the other side's argument, not the weakest or a straw man.
- **Respectful framing:** "I see it differently because..." / "the way I'd push back on that is..." — disagreeing without triggering defensiveness.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Rhetoric in practice:** establishing your own credibility briefly and honestly (ethos), making the stakes concrete and felt (pathos), keeping the logic airtight (logos) — used together in one argument.
- **Arguing both sides on command:** taking either side of a real issue with equal rigor and equal genuine effort, regardless of your actual belief.
- **Structured disagreement formats:** stating your position, stating the strongest opposing point (steelman, from Section 9), then explaining specifically why you still land where you do.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Persuasive delivery under pushback:** holding your structure, tone, and composure when interrupted, challenged, or asked a hostile follow-up — using Section 7's confidence and Section 5's regulation directly.
- **Live rebuttal speed:** generating a real-time counter to a new point you didn't prepare for, using the CREC spine on the fly.
- **Knowing when you're wrong:** genuinely updating your position mid-debate when the other side lands a point that actually holds up — the mark of real skill, not a weakness.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **CREC delivery drill:** given a topic, deliver a full claim-reason-evidence-conclusion argument in under 90 seconds.
- **Rebuttal drill:** given an argument (real or provided), rebut its strongest point directly.
- **Both-sides full debate:** argue one side of a topic for 2 minutes, then immediately argue the other side for 2 minutes, both at full effort.
- **Pushback drill:** deliver an argument while being interrupted with a hostile counter-question partway through, and recover the thread.

## 8. Drills
- **Rapid-rebuttal drill:** respond to 5 rapid-fire counter-points on the same topic, one after another, without long pauses.
- **Ethos-pathos-logos check:** after delivering an argument, identify which of the three levers you used, and add the one you left out.
- **Composure drill:** have someone (or yourself, scripted) interrupt you mid-argument with a sharp objection; practice acknowledging it and returning to your point without losing composure.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Use CREC delivery the next time you're asked your opinion on something substantive.
- Use structured disagreement ("I see the strongest case for X as..., but I land on Y because...") in your next real disagreement.
- Apply rebuttal-the-strongest-point the next time someone pushes back on you.

## 10. Challenges
- Have a real structured debate (with a friend, or as a solo both-sides exercise) on a genuinely contested topic, arguing to steelman standard on both sides.
- Deliberately seek out one real pushback or hostile question this week (a real disagreement, a tough question at work) and hold your composure and structure through it.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Given a real debatable topic, deliver (record/write) a 90-second argument for one side with no prep beyond picking the side. Send me the transcript — I'll assess structure, not just content.

**Exit (Day 10):** A full debate performance: argue one side of a real topic (2 min) with CREC structure, take and rebut a hostile pushback question, then argue the *other* side (2 min) at equal rigor.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Arguments are just assertions, no real reason/evidence chain; flustered by pushback |
| Developing | Can build a basic CREC argument with prep; struggles arguing the side they disagree with |
| Competent | Delivers structured arguments with real rebuttals; argues both sides with real effort |
| Advanced | Holds composure and structure under hostile pushback; rebuts live, unprepared points well |
| Highly Proficient | Persuasive and precise under real pressure; updates position genuinely when warranted |
| Mastery | Debates skillfully in real, unscripted, high-stakes conversation without it feeling like "debate mode" |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 11.

## 13. Day-by-Day Training Plan

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|

| Exercise: CREC drill x2 | 20 | 2 new topics, full CREC delivery, 90 sec each. |
| Reflection | 15 | Which part of CREC (claim, reason, evidence, conclusion) is weakest for you right now? |

| Segment | Min | What to do |
