# Module 6 — Video Ideas

## 1. Concept

Idea generation is a **system**, not a lightning-strike of inspiration. Professional creators aren't more creative than beginners on any given day — they have a repeatable process that produces a steady stream of ideas plus a filter that separates good ones from time-wasters. This module gives you both.

## 2. Why it matters

Running out of ideas is the single most common reason beginner channels go inconsistent, and inconsistency is the single most common reason channels never build momentum (Module 18). A system prevents "idea panic" the night before you planned to film.

## 3. Where ideas actually come from

- **Trend research**: what's currently spiking in your space — game releases/updates, viral clips, community drama, patch notes, esports/creator news. Sources: YouTube's own trending/gaming tab, X/Twitter gaming and clip accounts, Reddit communities for your games, Discord servers, other creators' comment sections (what are people asking for).
- **Evergreen ideas**: searchable year-round regardless of trend cycles — "how to beat [specific boss]," "is [game] worth it in [year]," "scariest moments in [game]," tier lists, challenge formats. These build a long-tail view base that trend content doesn't.
- **Viral concept adaptation**: not copying a specific video, but noticing a *format* that's working across the space (a challenge structure, a reaction format, a comedic bit style) and adapting it through your own personality/POV.
- **Competitor research**: watch what similar-sized and slightly-larger creators in your space are making that's performing well (check their video's relative view count vs. their channel average — a video that overperforms their norm is signal). Don't copy the video; identify *why* it worked and find your own angle on the same underlying interest.
- **Content gaps**: search your target game/topic on YouTube and note what's NOT being covered well, or what's covered but poorly (bad pacing, no personality, outdated). Gaps are highest-leverage because you face less competition for the same search/suggested traffic.
- **Combining concepts**: cross your content pillars (Module 3) — e.g., combine a reaction format with a gaming topic ("reacting to the worst plays in [game]'s community") to create something that's neither pure gaming nor pure reaction.
- **Improving existing ideas**: take a video you already made that did reasonably well and ask "what's the sequel, the escalation, the opposite version, the deeper dive?"

## 4. The idea-generation system (repeatable weekly process)

1. **Capture constantly**: keep one running idea list (notes app, doc, spreadadsheet) — anything from Section 3, the second you notice it. Don't filter at capture time.
2. **Batch review weekly** (pairs with Module 23): once a week, review the list, score every idea (Section 5), and move your top 3–5 into your actual content calendar.
3. **Keep an evergreen backlog**: always have 3–5 evergreen ideas ready to film on a day when nothing is trending — never let a trend drought stall your upload schedule.

## 5. Idea scoring system

Score each idea 1–5 on each axis, total out of 35:

```
IDEA: ___
Clickability (would the title/thumbnail earn a click?):        ___/5
Entertainment potential (can I make this genuinely fun?):      ___/5
Audience interest (does my specific audience care?):           ___/5
Originality (is my angle distinct from what's already out there?): ___/5
Competition (lower = more crowded; score inversely — 5 = low competition): ___/5
Trend potential (timing — is this hot now or fading?):         ___/5
Long-term value (will this still get views in 6 months?):      ___/5
TOTAL: ___/35
```

Rough guide: 25+ = film this soon. 18–24 = keep in backlog, refine the angle. Under 18 = probably not worth the production time right now.

Note the trade-off baked in: a high-trend, low-long-term-value idea (breaking news reaction) should be filmed fast or not at all — don't let it sit in the backlog past its relevance window. A high-long-term-value evergreen idea has no urgency — perfect for filling a slow week.

## 6. Examples

- Trend-driven: a major game update drops → same-day reaction/breakdown video, scored high on trend potential and clickability, low on long-term value — film within 24–48 hours or skip it.
- Evergreen: "ranking every [game] boss by how unfair they are" — scores high on long-term value and audience interest, low urgency, perfect backlog filler.
- Content gap: noticing every big channel covered a game's main story but nobody made a good "hidden secrets" video — lower competition score input, higher net score.

## 7. Exercises

1. Spend 20 minutes doing trend research (Section 3 sources) in your niche right now. Capture 10 raw ideas with no filtering.
2. Score all 10 using the Idea Scoring System.
3. Identify your top 3 and note, for each, whether it's time-sensitive (film this week) or evergreen (backlog).

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] I have a running idea-capture system I actually use (not just "I'll remember it")
- [ ] I score ideas before committing production time
- [ ] I always have at least 3 evergreen ideas in backlog for slow weeks
- [ ] I check competitor/trend sources at least weekly

## 10. Common mistakes

- Waiting for inspiration instead of running the weekly system.
- Filming every idea at a "9/10 excitement" gut feeling without scoring — excitement and audience interest aren't the same thing.
- Chasing every trend regardless of fit with your content pillars (Module 3) — leads back to the "random channel" problem.

## 11. What beginners waste time on

- Spending hours searching for "the one perfect idea" instead of capturing many and filtering with the scoring system.
- Ignoring evergreen ideas entirely because they feel less exciting than trend content — evergreen is what stabilizes your view count over time.

## 12. Homework

Build your first Weekly Idea Capture Sheet with at least 10 scored ideas. Select your next 3 videos to film from the highest scorers, mixing at least one trend-driven and one evergreen idea.

## 13. Checkpoint

You should have a scored list of 10+ ideas and be able to explain why your top 3 selections scored well. If your list is all trend-chasing with zero evergreen ideas (or vice versa), rebalance before advancing.
