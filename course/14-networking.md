# Section 14 — Networking

**Est. length:** 8–12 sessions (core track: 9 days).
**Prerequisite:** Section 6 (Social Skills), Section 7 (Confidence). **Feeds into:** Leadership (building allies/team).

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Meeting people, introducing yourself, starting professional conversations, following up, maintaining relationships over time, providing value first, building professional relationships and reputation, and avoiding purely transactional relationships.

## 2. Why It Matters
This is Social Skills and Confidence applied specifically to professional-relationship building — one of the most direct real-world outlets for nearly everything trained so far.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **Networking is relationship-building, not collecting contacts:** the goal is a small number of real, mutually valuable relationships, not a large list of people who vaguely know your name.
- **Give before you ask:** offering something of value (an introduction, information, help) before you need anything builds a relationship that can actually hold a future ask.
- **Follow-up is where networking actually happens:** the first conversation is just an opener; almost all the real relationship-building is in what happens after.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Self-introduction:** a short, clear statement of who you are and what you do/care about, without over-explaining or under-selling.
- **Starting a professional conversation:** situational openers (shared context, genuine question about their work) — reusing Section 6's opener skills in a professional frame.
- **Basic follow-up:** a short, specific message after meeting someone, referencing something particular from the conversation.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Providing value first:** identifying something genuinely useful you can offer someone (an introduction, a resource, help with something) before or instead of asking for anything.
- **Maintaining relationships over time:** light, periodic, genuine touch-points (not just reaching out when you need something) that keep a relationship warm.
- **Reading professional context:** adjusting your approach for a formal event, a casual professional setting, or an online connection.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Building reputation:** being known for something specific and reliable (consistency, expertise, generosity) that makes people want to introduce you to others.
- **Avoiding transactional relationships:** noticing when you're only reaching out to extract value, and correcting toward relationships that go both directions over time.
- **Strategic relationship-building:** thoughtfully identifying which relationships matter most for your actual goals, without treating people as means to an end.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **Self-introduction drill:** write and practice a 15-second self-introduction, then a 30-second version for more context.
- **Value-first drill:** for 2 people you know, identify one genuine piece of value you could offer them with no ask attached.
- **Follow-up drill:** write a specific, non-generic follow-up message referencing a real detail from a real conversation.
- **Reputation audit:** write what you'd want to be known for professionally, and whether your recent actions actually build that.

## 8. Drills
- **Meeting-new-people drill:** initiate one new professional-context conversation this week (event, online, introduction).
- **Touch-point drill:** send one genuine, no-ask check-in to an existing contact.
- **Transactional-check drill:** before reaching out to someone, ask honestly: am I offering anything, or only asking?

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Use the self-introduction the next time you meet someone new in a professional-adjacent context.
- Send one real value-first message this week to an existing contact.
- Follow up on your last new professional contact with a specific, genuine message.

## 10. Challenges
- Meet one genuinely new person this week (event, online community, introduction) and follow up meaningfully within 48 hours.
- Identify one relationship in your life that's become purely transactional and take one real step to make it more mutual.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Describe your current networking habits (if any): how often do you meet new professional contacts, and how often do you follow up? Recall your last new professional connection — what happened after the first conversation?

**Exit (Day 9):** Real-world task — meet or re-engage with one real person using a self-introduction, provide (or plan) something of genuine value, and send a specific, non-generic follow-up. Report the full sequence.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Avoids networking; no follow-up habit at all |
| Developing | Meets people occasionally but rarely follows up or offers value first |
| Competent | Reliably introduces self well, follows up specifically, offers value before asking |
| Advanced | Maintains relationships over time with genuine, non-transactional touch-points |
| Highly Proficient | Has a small network of real, mutually valuable relationships |
| Mastery | Builds and sustains a reputation that draws opportunities and introductions naturally |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 15.
