# Section 11 — Problem-Solving

**Length:** 7 days. **Goal:** a repeatable process for any real problem — course, trading, or life.

---

### Day 1 — Naming the Real Problem

The problem you first name is usually a symptom, not the cause. "I never have time" is vague enough to be unsolvable. Rewriting it as one specific, concrete sentence is most of the actual work.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: pick a real current problem. Write your instinctive first attempt to solve it, no framework. |
| 20 | Rewrite 3 vague complaints (including your baseline one) as specific, one-sentence problem statements. |
| 15 | How did rewriting the baseline problem change how you saw it? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — Finding the Root Cause

Ask "why" five times in a row on a problem, each answer feeding the next question. You'll usually land somewhere very different from where you started.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Reread yesterday's 3 problem statements. |
| 25 | Apply 5 Whys to 2 real recurring problems, including your baseline one. |
| 20 | Was the root cause surprising compared to your first instinct? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 3 — Breaking It Into Pieces

A big vague problem is paralyzing. The same problem split into 4–5 concrete sub-problems usually has an obvious next step.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's root cause. |
| 20 | Split your baseline problem's root cause into 4–5 concrete sub-problems. |
| 15 | Convert 5 vague complaints into specific, answerable questions. |
| 20 | Which sub-problem is actually highest-leverage to solve first? |

### Day 4 — Generating Options Before Judging Any

Judging ideas while you generate them kills the good ones early. Get 8 options down with zero filtering, then evaluate.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall your top sub-problem. |
| 35 | Generate 8 solutions to it with zero self-judgment. |
| 15 | Which of the 8 surprised you — one you wouldn't have reached if you'd judged as you went? |
| 5 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Choosing and Testing Cheaply

Score your options against real criteria — cost, effort, risk, reversibility — instead of gut-picking the first plausible one. Then test the winner as cheaply as possible before committing fully.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Reread yesterday's 8 solutions. |
| 20 | Score them against cost/effort/risk/reversibility. Pick a top choice. |
| 20 | Design the smallest, cheapest real test of that choice. |
| 15 | Would you have picked this option without the scoring step? |

### Day 6 — Seeing the Wider System, and a Harder Problem

A fix rarely happens in isolation — ask what else it affects. Then try the whole process on a genuinely messier problem, not a clean one.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall your cheap test plan. |
| 15 | List 2 side effects your chosen solution might create elsewhere. |
| 30 | Pick a genuinely messier, less-defined problem. Run problem-statement + 5 Whys + sub-problems on it. |
| 10 | What made the messier problem harder to pin down? |

### Day 7 — Exit Check

Run the full process end to end on a new, moderately messy real problem: statement, root cause, sub-problems, 5+ options, evaluation, cheap test. Send it to me.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 40 | The full exit task. |
| 15 | Compare to Day 1's first-instinct approach — what's structurally different now? |

**Mastery bar:** you find root cause, break problems down, and generate multiple real options before choosing — automatically, not just when prompted. If not yet, repeat root-cause and evaluation on a fresh problem.
