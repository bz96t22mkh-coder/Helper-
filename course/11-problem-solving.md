# Section 11 — Problem-Solving

**Length:** 7 days. **Goal:** a repeatable process for any real problem — course, trading, or life.

---

### Day 1 — Naming the Real Problem

The problem you first name is usually a symptom, not the cause. "I never have time" is vague enough to be unsolvable — you can't act on it. Rewriting it as one specific, concrete sentence is most of the actual work: "I lose about an hour every evening to phone scrolling instead of my planned study block."

**How to do it:** take your vague complaint and keep asking "specifically, what does that look like?" until you land on something you could point a camera at.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: pick a real current problem. Write your instinctive first attempt to solve it, no framework. |
| 20 | Rewrite 3 vague complaints (including your baseline one) as specific, one-sentence problem statements. |
| 15 | How did rewriting the baseline problem change how you saw it? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — Finding the Root Cause

Ask "why" five times in a row on a problem, each answer feeding the next question. You'll usually land somewhere very different from where you started.

**Worked example.** "I keep missing my study block." Why? "I'm too tired by then." Why? "I stay up late." Why? "I'm on my phone in bed." Why? "It's the easiest thing to reach for when I'm bored at night." Why? "I don't have anything else set up to do instead." Five whys later, the real problem isn't "time management" — it's "no default wind-down activity besides the phone."

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Reread yesterday's 3 problem statements. |
| 25 | Apply 5 Whys to 2 real recurring problems, including your baseline one. |
| 20 | Was the root cause surprising compared to your first instinct? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 3 — Breaking It Into Pieces

A big vague problem is paralyzing. The same problem split into 4–5 concrete sub-problems usually has an obvious next step for at least one of them.

**How to do it:** write the root cause at the top of a page. Ask "what are the separate pieces that make this true?" and list them as their own smaller problems — each one should feel solvable on its own, even if the whole thing didn't.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's root cause. |
| 20 | Split your baseline problem's root cause into 4–5 concrete sub-problems. |
| 20 | Convert 5 vague complaints into specific, answerable questions. |
| 15 | Which sub-problem is actually highest-leverage to solve first? |

### Day 4 — Generating Options Before Judging Any

Judging ideas while you generate them kills the good ones early — the moment you think "that's dumb" about idea #3, you stop reaching for idea #4, which might have been the real answer. Get 8 options down with zero filtering, then evaluate afterward, as a separate step.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall your top sub-problem. |
| 35 | Generate 8 solutions to it with zero self-judgment — write down even the bad or embarrassing ones. |
| 15 | Which of the 8 surprised you — one you wouldn't have reached if you'd judged as you went? |
| 5 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Choosing and Testing Cheaply

Score your options against real criteria — cost, effort, risk, reversibility — instead of gut-picking the first plausible one. Then test the winner as cheaply as possible before committing fully: what's the smallest version of this you could try in a day, not a month?

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Reread yesterday's 8 solutions. |
| 20 | Score them against cost/effort/risk/reversibility. Pick a top choice. |
| 20 | Design the smallest, cheapest real test of that choice. |
| 15 | Would you have picked this option without the scoring step? |

### Day 6 — Seeing the Wider System, and a Harder Problem

A fix rarely happens in isolation — ask what else it affects, positively or negatively. Then try the whole process on a genuinely messier problem, one where even the "real problem" isn't obvious yet.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall your cheap test plan. |
| 15 | List 2 side effects your chosen solution might create elsewhere. |
| 30 | Pick a genuinely messier, less-defined problem. Run problem-statement + 5 Whys + sub-problems on it. |
| 10 | What made the messier problem harder to pin down? |

### Day 7 — Exit Check

Run the full process end to end on a new, moderately messy real problem: statement, root cause, sub-problems, 5+ options, evaluation, cheap test. Send it to me.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 40 | The full exit task. |
| 15 | Compare to Day 1's first-instinct approach — what's structurally different now? |

**Mastery bar:** you find root cause, break problems down, and generate multiple real options before choosing — automatically, not just when prompted. If not yet, repeat root-cause and evaluation on a fresh problem.
