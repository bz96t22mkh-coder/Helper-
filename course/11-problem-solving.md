# Section 11 — Problem-Solving

**Est. length:** 6–10 sessions (core track: 7 days).
**Prerequisite:** Section 9 (Critical Thinking). **Feeds into:** Decision-Making, Strategic Thinking.

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Problem definition, root-cause analysis, breaking problems down, asking better questions, generating solutions, evaluating solutions, testing solutions, and basic systems thinking.

## 2. Why It Matters
Most bad outcomes trace back to solving the wrong problem, or the symptom instead of the cause. This section builds a repeatable process you can point at any real problem — course-related, trading-related, or personal.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **The problem you first name is rarely the real problem:** initial framing is often a symptom ("I can't focus") rather than the cause ("my environment is full of triggers" or "the task is unclear"). Defining the problem well is most of the work.
- **Breaking a problem down makes it solvable:** a big, vague problem is paralyzing; the same problem split into concrete sub-parts usually has an obvious next step.
- **Generating and evaluating are separate steps:** judging ideas while generating them kills good options early — separate the brainstorm from the filter.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Problem statement:** writing the problem as one clear, specific sentence before doing anything else.
- **5 Whys:** repeatedly asking "why" on a problem to move from symptom to root cause.
- **Basic solution brainstorm:** generating multiple options (not just the first idea) before picking one.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Breaking problems into sub-problems:** splitting a big, vague problem into smaller, concretely solvable pieces.
- **Asking better questions:** reframing "why isn't this working" into specific, answerable questions ("what exactly fails, when, and for whom").
- **Evaluating solutions:** weighing options against clear criteria (cost, effort, risk, reversibility) instead of gut-picking the first plausible one.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Testing solutions cheaply:** running a small, low-cost test of a solution before committing fully (a mental model directly reusable in trading and in life decisions).
- **Systems thinking basics:** seeing a problem as part of a system with feedback loops and side effects, not an isolated issue — asking "what does fixing this affect elsewhere?"
- **Progressively harder, less-defined problems:** applying the full process to messy, ambiguous real problems, not just clean textbook ones.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **Problem-statement drill:** take a vague complaint ("I'm bad with money," "I never have time") and rewrite it as one specific, solvable problem statement.
- **5 Whys drill:** take a real recurring problem and ask "why" 5 times to find the root cause.
- **Brainstorm-then-filter drill:** generate 8 solutions to a real problem with zero judgment, then apply evaluation criteria only afterward.
- **Cheap-test drill:** design the smallest, cheapest possible test of a proposed solution before full commitment.

## 8. Drills
- **Sub-problem split drill:** take one large, vague goal/problem and split it into 4–5 concrete sub-problems in under 10 minutes.
- **Better-question drill:** convert 5 vague complaints into specific, answerable questions.
- **Systems-ripple drill:** for a real solution you're considering, list 2 side effects it might create elsewhere.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Apply the 5 Whys to a real recurring frustration (in this course, trading study, or daily life).
- Use brainstorm-then-filter the next time you face a real decision with multiple options.
- Use a cheap test before fully committing to your next non-trivial change or purchase.

## 10. Challenges
- Take a real, currently-unsolved problem in your life and run the full process end to end: problem statement → root cause → sub-problems → brainstorm → evaluate → cheap test.
- Take a genuinely messy, ill-defined problem (not a clean textbook one) and apply the same process, noting where it got harder.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Pick a real, current problem you have. Write your instinctive first attempt at solving it (no framework yet) — what's the problem, and what would you do? Send me this first-instinct version.

**Exit (Day 7):** Take a new, real, moderately messy problem. Run the full process: problem statement, root-cause (5 Whys), sub-problems, brainstormed options (min. 5), evaluation against criteria, and a cheap test plan for your top choice.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Jumps straight to a solution for the first-named (often wrong) problem |
| Developing | Can define a problem and brainstorm with prompting; skips root-cause analysis |
| Competent | Reliably finds root cause, breaks problems down, generates multiple options before choosing |
| Advanced | Evaluates against real criteria and designs cheap tests before full commitment |
| Highly Proficient | Applies systems thinking — anticipates side effects and downstream ripples |
| Mastery | The full process runs automatically on any real problem, even messy/ambiguous ones |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 12.

## 13. Day-by-Day Training Plan

### Day 1 — Baseline + Problem Definition (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline assessment | 15 | Complete the Section 11 baseline task. |
| Learn | 10 | Read Section 3–4 (symptom vs. real problem, problem statements). |
| Exercise: Problem-statement drill x3 | 20 | Rewrite 3 vague complaints as specific, one-sentence problem statements. |
| Reflection | 15 | How did rewriting the baseline problem this way change how you saw it? |

### Day 2 — Root-Cause Analysis (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Reread yesterday's 3 problem statements. |
| Learn | 10 | Read 5 Whys (Section 4). |
| Exercise: 5 Whys drill x2 | 25 | Apply 5 Whys to 2 real recurring problems, including your baseline problem. |
| Reflection | 20 | Was the root cause surprising compared to your first instinct? |

### Day 3 — Breaking Problems Down & Better Questions (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Recall yesterday's root cause. |
| Learn | 10 | Read sub-problem splitting, better questions (Section 5). |
| Exercise: Sub-problem split drill | 20 | Split your baseline problem's root cause into 4–5 concrete sub-problems. |
| Exercise: Better-question drill | 15 | Convert 5 vague complaints into specific, answerable questions. |
| Reflection | 10 | Which sub-problem is actually the highest-leverage one to solve first? |

### Day 4 — Generating Solutions (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Recall your top sub-problem. |
| Learn | 5 | Reread generate-then-filter (Section 3). |
| Exercise: Brainstorm-then-filter drill | 35 | Generate 8 solutions to your top sub-problem with zero self-judgment, then set it aside. |
| Reflection | 15 | Which of the 8 surprised you — an idea you wouldn't have reached if you'd judged as you went? |

### Day 5 — Evaluating & Testing Solutions (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Reread yesterday's 8 solutions. |
| Learn | 10 | Read evaluation criteria and cheap testing (Section 5–6). |
| Exercise: Evaluation matrix | 20 | Score your 8 solutions against cost/effort/risk/reversibility; pick a top choice. |
| Exercise: Cheap-test design | 20 | Design the smallest, cheapest real test of your top choice. |
| Reflection | 5 | Would you have picked this option without the evaluation step? |

### Day 6 — Systems Thinking + Harder Problem (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 5 | Recall your cheap test plan. |
| Learn | 10 | Read systems thinking basics (Section 6). |
| Exercise: Systems-ripple drill | 15 | List 2 side effects your chosen solution might create elsewhere. |
| Exercise: Harder problem | 25 | Pick a genuinely messier, less-defined problem and run problem-statement + 5 Whys + sub-problems on it. |
| Reflection | 5 | What made the messier problem harder to pin down? |

### Day 7 — Integration & Exit Assessment (60 min)

| Segment | Min | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 | Recall the full process steps. |
| Exit assessment | 40 | Run the Section 11 exit task end to end on a new, moderately messy real problem. Send me the full breakdown. |
| Evaluation | — | Scored against the mastery table. |
| Reflection | 15 | Compare to Day 1's first-instinct approach — what's structurally different now? |

**If below Competent:** extend 2–3 days repeating root-cause and evaluation steps on a fresh problem with tighter feedback. **If Competent+:** move to Section 12; this problem-solving process becomes the default first step whenever Decision-Making needs options generated.
