# Section 16 — Strategic Thinking

**Length:** 9 days. **Goal:** think past the first, obvious effect of a decision — applies to career, learning path, and trading alike.

---

### Day 1 — Rebuilding From Fundamentals

Most "shoulds" are just convention, not truth. Ask whether an assumption is actually true, or just how it's normally done, and rebuild your view from what's actually real underneath it.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 15 | Baseline: describe a real long-term goal you're pursuing. Is your thinking about it mostly next-step, or does it consider ripple effects and multiple futures? |
| 20 | Take a belief about how something "should" be done. Rebuild it from fundamentals. |
| 15 | Did the fundamentals actually support the conventional approach, or not? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 2 — And Then What Happens?

Most strategic mistakes come from stopping at the first, obvious effect. Ask "and then what happens?" three times in a row and you'll usually find the real cost or benefit.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall yesterday's rebuild. |
| 25 | For 2 real plans or decisions, ask "and then what happens?" three times in a row each. |
| 20 | Where did the second- or third-order effect change how good the plan actually looks? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 3 — Your Goals Are One System, Not Separate Tracks

This course, trading, and career aren't independent — they feed or drain each other. Seeing the connections changes how you'd actually allocate time between them.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | Sketch how your current goals interconnect and feed or drain each other. |
| 15 | Where's a hidden feedback loop, positive or negative, you hadn't noticed? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 4 — What to Stop Doing

Choosing what *not* to do matters as much as choosing what to do. Trying to be good at everything usually means being mediocre at everything.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 25 | List 3 things you could stop doing to free capacity for higher-leverage work. |
| 20 | What's stopping you from actually cutting one of these? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 5 — Checkpoint

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall this week's tools. |
| 30 | Take a real decision. Apply fundamentals-thinking to one assumption and map 2 second-order effects. |
| 15 | Rep on weakest element. |
| 10 | Progress vs. Day 1. |

### Day 6 — Best Case, Worst Case, Most Likely

Sketch 2–3 plausible futures for a real strategic choice and check whether your plan actually survives the worst one, not just the one you're hoping for.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 30 | For a real strategic choice, sketch best/worst/most-likely scenarios. Check your plan against each. |
| 15 | Does your current plan survive the worst case, or does it assume best case? |
| 10 | Reflection. |

### Day 7 — Where Small Effort Pays Off Disproportionately

Some time/effort produces linear returns; some produces outsized returns because it compounds — a skill, a relationship, a piece of knowledge. Find where you're spending effort without leverage.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 20 | List your current time and effort allocations. Identify which are high-leverage vs. merely busy. |
| 20 | Compare where your time actually went this week vs. your stated priorities. |
| 15 | What's one reallocation you'll make starting this week? |

### Day 8 — Combining All Three Lenses

Fundamentals, second-order thinking, and systems thinking together, on one genuinely complex question — not one lens at a time.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up. |
| 35 | Take a genuinely complex real strategic question — career, balancing your courses, a real financial decision. Reason through it using all three lenses together. |
| 15 | What did combining the lenses surface that a single lens would have missed? |
| 5 | Reflection. |

### Day 9 — Exit Check

Apply fundamentals-thinking to one assumption, map second-order effects, sketch 3 scenarios, and name your highest-leverage next move — on a real current strategic question. Send it to me.

| Min | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Recall the full toolkit. |
| 40 | The full exit task. |
| 15 | Compare to Day 1 — how first-order vs. strategic was your original thinking? |

**Mastery bar:** you map second-order effects and question assumptions on real decisions without being prompted. If not yet, repeat second-order mapping and scenario planning on a fresh real question.
