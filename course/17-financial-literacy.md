# Section 17 — Financial Literacy

**Est. length:** 12–20 sessions (core track: 14 days).
**Prerequisite:** none strictly (independent of the communication chain — can be moved earlier if you'd rather interleave it with day-trading study; say so and I'll reorder). **Feeds into:** applies Decision-Making (risk/EV) and Strategic Thinking (resource allocation) directly.

**Important:** this section is educational, building general financial literacy and vocabulary. It is not personalized financial, investment, or tax advice — for real decisions with real money, especially anything beyond the basics, verify with current, qualified sources.

---

## 1. What You Need to Learn
Budgeting, saving, emergency funds, debt, interest and compound interest, inflation, banking, credit, investing fundamentals (stocks, bonds, ETFs), risk and diversification, net worth, cash flow, assets vs. liabilities, financial planning basics, and recognizing financial scams.

## 2. Why It Matters
Financial literacy is a foundational life skill and directly complements your day-trading study — this section builds the general financial vocabulary and mental models (many from Decision-Making and Strategic Thinking) that trading-specific knowledge sits on top of.

## 3. Foundational Concepts
- **Net worth = assets − liabilities:** the single clearest snapshot of your actual financial position, more useful than income alone.
- **Cash flow = money in − money out over time:** distinct from net worth — you can have positive net worth and negative cash flow, or vice versa.
- **Compound interest works for you (savings/investing) or against you (debt) — the same math, opposite direction:** understanding this one mechanism explains most of personal finance.

## 4. Beginner Concepts
- **Assets vs. liabilities:** assets put money in your pocket or hold/grow value; liabilities take money out or owe money.
- **Budgeting basics:** tracking income vs. expenses and deciding allocations deliberately (e.g., needs/wants/savings splits) rather than reactively.
- **Saving & emergency funds:** setting aside money for planned goals and an emergency buffer (commonly 3–6 months of essential expenses) before other financial goals.

## 5. Intermediate Concepts
- **Debt and interest:** the difference between "good" debt (can build an asset/earning capacity, reasonable rate) and "bad" debt (high-interest, funds depreciating consumption); how interest rates compound your balance over time if unpaid.
- **Inflation:** money loses purchasing power over time, which is why cash sitting idle long-term quietly loses real value — a key reason saving alone isn't the same as investing.
- **Banking & credit basics:** how checking/savings accounts, credit scores, and credit reports work, and why credit history matters for future borrowing.
- **Investing fundamentals:** what a stock (ownership in a company), a bond (a loan to a company/government), and an ETF (a bundled basket of many stocks/bonds) actually are, in plain terms.

## 6. Advanced Concepts
- **Risk and diversification:** not putting all your money in one asset/company, because uncorrelated assets reduce the chance of a single event wiping you out — the core logic behind diversification.
- **Financial planning basics:** connecting short-term budgeting to medium-term saving goals to long-term investing/retirement thinking, as one continuous plan rather than separate silos.
- **Recognizing financial scams:** common red flags (guaranteed high returns, pressure to act immediately, unlicensed/unverifiable sources, "too good to be true" framing) — especially relevant given you're also studying trading, a space scammers target heavily.

## 7. Practical Exercises
- **Net worth statement:** list your actual assets and liabilities and calculate your current net worth.
- **Cash flow tracker:** track a real week or month of income vs. expenses.
- **Budget build:** design a simple needs/wants/savings budget split based on your real numbers.
- **Compound interest calculation:** manually work through how a sum grows over time at a given rate (savings) and how debt grows if unpaid (a credit card balance) — see the same math both directions.

## 8. Drills
- **Asset-or-liability drill:** for 10 real items/expenses in your life, classify each as asset or liability.
- **Good-debt-bad-debt sort:** for 3 examples of debt (real or hypothetical), classify as generally "good" or "bad" and explain why.
- **Scam-spotting drill:** review 3 real or example financial pitches/offers and identify red flags.

## 9. Real-World Applications
- Build your actual net worth statement and cash flow tracker with real numbers.
- Set (or check) your actual emergency fund target based on your real essential expenses.
- Apply the scam red-flag checklist to anything trading- or investing-related you encounter.

## 10. Challenges
- Build a complete, real personal budget and 12-month financial snapshot (net worth + cash flow + savings goal), using your actual numbers.
- Explain, in your own words (using Section 3's teach-it drill), the difference between a stock, a bond, and an ETF to an imagined beginner.

## 11. Assessments

**Baseline (Day 1):** Rate your current financial knowledge (1–5) on: budgeting, debt/interest, investing basics. Do you currently know your own net worth or track your cash flow? Write your honest current state.

**Exit (Day 14):** Build a complete real financial snapshot: net worth statement, a month of cash flow, a working budget, an emergency fund target, and a one-paragraph explanation (teach-it style) of how compound interest, diversification, and one investment vehicle (stock/bond/ETF) work, in your own words.

## 12. Mastery Criteria

| Level | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginner | No budget, no net worth awareness, unclear on interest/investing basics |
| Developing | Has a rough sense of income/expenses; investing/debt concepts still fuzzy |
| Competent | Tracks net worth and cash flow; understands debt, interest, and basic investing vehicles clearly |
| Advanced | Has a working budget and emergency fund plan; understands risk/diversification concretely |
| Highly Proficient | Runs an integrated financial plan (budget → savings → investing) and can explain it clearly to others |
| Mastery | Financial literacy is applied automatically to real decisions, including trading-adjacent ones |

Competent+ needed to progress to Section 18.
