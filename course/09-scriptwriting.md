# Module 9 — Scriptwriting

## 1. Concept

A YouTube script for this niche is rarely a word-for-word transcript. It's a **structural map** — the hook, the beats, the transitions, the planned jokes/setups, and the ending — that you deliver conversationally rather than read. Think outline-with-key-lines, not stage play.

## 2. Why it matters

Unstructured filming produces unstructured, meandering videos that die on retention (Module 11). But over-scripted, fully-written delivery sounds robotic and kills the "personality-driven" feel you're going for (Module 2). The skill is structuring enough to guarantee pacing while leaving room to talk naturally (Module 10).

## 3. Universal structural elements

- **Hook (first 5–15 seconds)**: state or imply the payoff/stakes immediately. No slow intros, no "hey guys welcome back" before the hook.
- **Open loop**: a question or promise planted early that isn't resolved until later — keeps people watching to get the resolution.
- **Setup**: give the viewer only what they need to understand what's about to happen (context, rules, stakes) — efficiently, not exhaustively.
- **Payoff**: deliver on what the hook/open loop promised. Payoffs that don't match their setup break trust (see Module 7's clickbait warning).
- **Transitions**: a planned line or beat that moves from one segment to the next without an awkward dead stop — "but that wasn't even the worst part" style bridges.
- **Pacing**: alternate high-energy and lower-energy beats; back-to-back peak energy exhausts viewers, back-to-back low energy loses them.
- **Pattern interrupts**: a planned change — tone shift, sudden question to camera, unexpected aside — placed roughly every 60–90 seconds to reset attention (Module 11).
- **Calls to action (CTAs)**: light-touch, tied to content ("comment your prediction," "subscribe if you also think this boss is unfair") rather than a generic bolted-on ask.
- **Ending**: a deliberate close — a final joke, a takeaway, or (for series) a cliffhanger — not just fading out.

## 4. Format-specific templates

### Gaming video (general)
```
[0:00] Hook — the standout moment or stakes of this session, stated or teased
[0:05–0:20] Quick setup — what game, what I'm attempting, why it matters today
[Body] Play through planned beats (Module 4) — narrate honestly, insert planned bits at natural points, let genuine reactions breathe
[Recurring pattern interrupt] Every ~90s: a joke, a direct-to-camera aside, or a callback
[Ending] Resolution of the open loop + close (result, reflection, or cliffhanger) + CTA
```

### Horror gaming
```
[0:00] Hook — tease the scariest moment coming, without showing it
[Setup] What the game is, why it has a reputation, my own fear level going in
[Body] Alternate tension-building silence with commentary; narrate fear in real time
[Peak scare] Let it breathe — don't talk over the reaction itself
[Ending] Comedic decompression + close + CTA
```

### Funny/chaotic gaming
```
[0:00] Hook — funniest moment teased or best line delivered cold
[Setup] Minimal — get to the chaos fast
[Body] High commentary density; build joke chains/callbacks
[Ending] Biggest laugh saved for last where possible + CTA
```

### Challenge gaming
```
[0:00] Hook — state the challenge and the stakes immediately
[Setup] Explain the rule clearly (viewers must understand win/lose condition)
[Body] Attempts escalate in tension; narrate the mental state at each attempt
[Ending] Result (win/lose) + honest reflection + CTA
```

### Reaction video (general — pairs with Module 5 structures)
```
[0:00] Hook — my overall take or the most surprising beat, teased
[Setup] Brief context on the source material
[Body] React through material with commentary/opinion/prediction layered in (Module 5 toolbox); pause for 1–2 deeper takes
[Ending] Resolve my opening take/prediction + closing opinion + CTA
```

### Commentary/reaction (analysis-heavy)
```
[0:00] Hook — state the debate/question I'll answer
[Body] Walk through evidence/clips in segments, give a take after each
[Ending] Final verdict resolving the opening question + CTA
```

### Story-based video
```
[0:00] Hook — the emotional high point or twist, teased without spoiling
[Setup] Establish characters/stakes quickly
[Body] Build rising action with narrated internal reactions; plant and pay off open loops
[Ending] Resolution + emotional closing beat + CTA
```

## 5. How much to write out vs. leave loose

- **Always fully write**: the hook (first line matters too much to improvise cold), the CTA (easy to forget or fumble), and any joke/line you know needs precise wording to land.
- **Bullet only**: setup/context beats — know your points, say them in your own words.
- **Leave fully open**: real-time reactions, gameplay commentary, anything that needs to be genuine (over-scripting these kills authenticity — see Module 10).

## 6. Examples

- A horror-game hook scripted word-for-word: "There's a room in this game that made me turn my monitor off. We're going there right now." — precise, because the wording of a hook matters too much to improvise.
- A reaction video's setup left as bullets: "• what this clip is • why it's blowing up • my one-line initial take" — hit these points in your own words each take.

## 7. Exercises

1. Pick a planned video and write it using the matching format template from Section 4 — full hook and CTA, bullets for setup, open for the body.
2. Read your hook out loud 3 times. If it takes more than ~10 seconds to say, tighten it.
3. Identify where you'll place at least 2 pattern interrupts in the body.

## 8. Templates

Use the format-specific templates in Section 4 directly as fill-in skeletons.

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] Hook is fully scripted and under ~15 seconds
- [ ] At least one open loop planted early
- [ ] Setup is bullet-only, not fully scripted
- [ ] At least 2 pattern interrupts planned in the body
- [ ] Ending resolves the open loop and includes a content-tied CTA

## 10. Common mistakes

- Fully scripting gameplay commentary word-for-word, producing stiff, unnatural delivery.
- No open loop — the video has no reason to keep watching past curiosity about the topic itself.
- Slow, unscripted intros before the hook ("hey guys, welcome back to the channel, today we're gonna...") — cut this, start with the hook.
- Forgetting the CTA or making it generic and disconnected from the content.

## 11. What beginners waste time on

- Writing a full word-for-word script for the entire video, then sounding robotic reading it back, then blaming their on-camera skills instead of their scripting approach.
- Skipping scripting entirely and hoping the hook "just happens" in the moment — it rarely does under filming pressure; script the hook.

## 12. Homework

Write a complete script using the appropriate Section 4 template for your next planned video, following the "always write / bullet only / leave open" rule from Section 5.

## 13. Checkpoint

Read your scripted hook and CTA out loud. They should sound natural, not stiff, and take no more than ~15 seconds combined intro impact. If they sound written-not-spoken, revise using shorter sentences and your actual speaking rhythm from Module 10.
