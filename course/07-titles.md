# Module 7 — Titles

## 1. Concept

A title's only job is to earn a click from someone who's already interested enough to be shown your thumbnail (via search or browse). It does this by creating a **gap between what the viewer knows and what they want to know** — curiosity — or by matching **exactly what someone is searching for** — intent. Every effective title is doing one or both.

## 2. Why it matters

Referring back to Module 1: CTR is the mechanism by which YouTube decides whether to expand your video's reach. A great video with a flat title dies in obscurity. Titles are leverage — minutes of work with outsized impact on whether anyone ever sees the rest of your work.

## 3. The two title modes

- **Browse titles** (for suggested/home page — someone wasn't looking for this, you have to create the want): lean on curiosity, emotion, stakes, open loops.
- **Search titles** (for search traffic — someone already wants this, you have to match their exact phrasing): lean on specificity and the exact terms people type ("[game] all secret endings," "[game] worst boss explained").

Know which mode a given video is mostly serving (a trend reaction is mostly browse/suggested; a "how to beat X" guide is mostly search) and weight your title accordingly — though a great title often works both.

## 4. Title psychology toolbox

- **Curiosity/open loop**: state that something happened without revealing what ("I tried the hardest challenge in [game] and it broke me").
- **Specificity**: vague titles underperform specific ones — "This Boss Is Unfair" is weaker than "This [Game] Boss Killed Me 47 Times."
- **Stakes**: make clear something is at risk (a challenge, a reputation, a losing streak) — "If I Lose This I Delete The Game."
- **Emotion**: name the feeling directly when it's strong — fear, shock, betrayal, hype.
- **Mystery/story**: imply a narrative with a beginning and an unresolved end — "I Wasn't Supposed To Find This Room In [Game]."
- **Reaction-specific framing**: "Reacting to..." works but is generic on its own — pair it with your take ("Reacting to [Thing] and I Think Everyone's Wrong About It").

## 5. Practical process: generate 10–20, pick the strongest

For every video, don't settle on the first title you think of. Brainstorm 10–20 variants mixing the psychology levers above, then evaluate against this checklist:
- Does it create genuine curiosity or match real search intent (not both required, but at least one)?
- Is it specific rather than vague?
- Does it accurately represent the video (no bait you don't deliver on — see retention/trust cost below)?
- Is it under ~60 characters where possible so it doesn't truncate on mobile?
- Does it sound like something a person would say, not a keyword-stuffed string?

## 6. The clickbait line

Curiosity-driven titles only work long-term if the video delivers on the implied promise. A title that overpromises and underdelivers tanks retention (viewers leave immediately, feeling misled) which then tanks future distribution (Module 1) — clickbait is a short-term CTR trick that's a long-term retention and trust tax. The rule: the title can create a *question*; the video must *answer it honestly*, even if the answer is more nuanced or less dramatic than the title implied.

## 7. Examples

**Weak → Strong rewrites:**
- "Playing [Game] Part 3" → "I Wasn't Ready For What Happens At The End Of [Game]"
- "Reacting to a viral video" → "This Viral Clip Has Everyone Fighting — Here's What Actually Happened"
- "[Game] is hard" → "I Rage Quit [Game] 6 Times Before Beating This One Boss"

- [ ] Reads like natural speech, not a keyword string

## 11. Common mistakes

- Locking in the first title idea instead of generating a real batch.
- Clickbait that doesn't pay off — kills retention and repeat viewership.
- Vague titles ("Crazy Moment in [Game]") that create no specific curiosity gap.
- Ignoring search intent entirely on videos that have real search demand (guides, boss fights, "is it worth it" content).

## 12. What beginners waste time on

- Obsessing over keyword density instead of writing a title a real person would actually want to click.
- Changing a title repeatedly after upload hoping for a different result instead of diagnosing via Module 1 metrics whether it's actually a title problem or a thumbnail/retention problem.
