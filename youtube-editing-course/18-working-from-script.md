# Module 18 — Working From a Script / Video Plan

Every module so far has been about *how* to edit a moment. This module is about making sure, before any of that starts, you understand *what the whole video is trying to do* — because the same raw footage can be cut into a very different video depending on what the creator actually intended it to be.

## What to understand before editing

Whatever the creator provides ahead of a video (a full script, a loose outline, a one-line concept, or just a title/thumbnail idea — Module 19 covers getting this from them properly), extract:

- **The main idea** — in one sentence, what is this video actually about?
- **The hook** — what's the intended attention-grabbing angle (this may already be decided by the creator, or may be your job to find in the footage per Module 17)?
- **Important story beats** — what moments does the creator consider essential, that must survive the edit no matter what gets cut elsewhere?
- **Jokes** — if specific bits were planned in advance (a running gag, a setup-and-payoff), know what the intended payoff is so you don't accidentally cut the setup that makes it land, or bury the payoff under other content.
- **Expected payoff** — what is this video building to, and does the raw footage actually deliver it? If not, that's worth flagging to the creator (Module 19) rather than quietly cutting around the gap.
- **Title** — the title sets viewer expectations before they even click; the edit should deliver on what the title promises, or the video will feel like a mismatch (and risks harming retention/trust even with good editing).
- **Thumbnail concept** — similarly, if a specific moment is planned to be the thumbnail, that moment usually deserves real emphasis inside the video itself (it's already been promoted as a highlight — the edit should treat it like one).

## The question to answer before touching the timeline

**"What is this video trying to make the viewer feel?"** Every module's techniques exist to serve *some* feeling — funny, tense, hyped, shocked, nostalgic, satisfied. Knowing the intended feeling up front changes real editing decisions: a video meant to feel like a chaotic, high-energy chase through a game calls for denser pacing and more pattern interrupts (Modules 8–9, 16) than a video meant to feel like a slow-burn suspenseful challenge, even if both use footage from the same game.

## Working without a full script

Most Gaming + Reaction content isn't scripted line-by-line, and that's fine — this module doesn't require a formal document every time. What it requires is that before you start cutting, you can answer the questions above in your own words, even briefly. If the creator hasn't given you enough to answer them, that's the signal to ask (Module 19's communication system exists specifically for this) rather than guessing and editing a version of the video the creator didn't actually intend.
