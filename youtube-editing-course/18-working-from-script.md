# Module 18 — Working From a Script / Video Plan

> **In short:** Before you touch the timeline, understand what the whole video is trying to do. The same footage can become a very different video depending on the creator's intent.

Every module so far has been about *how* to edit one moment. This module is about making sure you understand *what the whole video is trying to do*, before any of that starts.

## What to understand before editing

Whatever the creator gives you before a video — a full script, a loose outline, a one-line idea, or just a title and thumbnail concept (Module 19 covers getting this from them properly) — pull out these things:

- **The main idea** — in one sentence, what is this video actually about?
- **The hook** — what's the plan for grabbing attention? (This may already be decided, or it may be your job to find it in the footage — Module 17.)
- **The must-keep moments** — what does the creator consider essential, that has to survive the edit no matter what else gets cut?
- **The jokes** — if a bit was planned in advance (a running gag, a setup-and-payoff), know the intended payoff, so you don't accidentally cut the setup that makes it work, or bury the payoff.
- **The payoff** — what is this video building toward? Does the raw footage actually deliver it? If not, that's worth telling the creator (Module 19), not quietly editing around.
- **The title** — it sets what the viewer expects before they even click. Your edit should deliver on what the title promises.
- **The thumbnail idea** — if a specific moment is planned as the thumbnail, that moment usually deserves real emphasis in the video — it's already been promoted as a highlight.

## The one question to answer first

**"What is this video trying to make the viewer feel?"** Every technique in this course serves *some* feeling — funny, tense, hyped, shocked, nostalgic, satisfied. Knowing the intended feeling up front changes real decisions. A video meant to feel like a chaotic, high-energy chase calls for denser pacing and more pattern interrupts (Modules 8–9, 16) than a video meant to feel like a slow-burn, suspenseful challenge — even using footage from the same game.

## You don't always need a full script

Most Gaming and Reaction content isn't written out line by line, and that's fine. This module doesn't require a formal document every time. What it requires is that, before you start cutting, you can answer the questions above in your own words — even briefly. If the creator hasn't given you enough to answer them, that's your signal to ask (Module 19's communication system exists for exactly this) instead of guessing and editing a video the creator didn't actually intend.
