# Module 20 — Complete Editing Workflow

> **In short:** This is the full process, start to finish, that everything in Modules 1–19 leads into. Keep this page open until it's second nature.

```
 1. Receive footage
        ↓
 2. Receive title / thumbnail / video idea (Editor Brief — Module 19)
        ↓
 3. Understand the story (Module 18 — what should the viewer feel?)
        ↓
 4. Watch the whole footage (take timestamp notes — Module 13/14 tagging)
        ↓
 5. Tag the important moments (funny, important, reaction, joke, story, suspense, boring, repetitive, unexpected)
        ↓
 6. Build the rough cut (the story's shape, in order, unpolished)
        ↓
 7. Cut the boring footage (Module 16)
        ↓
 8. Fix the pacing (Module 16 — rhythm, not just speed)
        ↓
 9. Add comedic cuts (Module 8)
        ↓
10. Add zooms (Module 9)
        ↓
11. Add memes / reaction images (Module 10)
        ↓
12. Add sound effects (Module 11)
        ↓
13. Add music (Module 12)
        ↓
14. Add text / captions where useful (Module 6)
        ↓
15. Watch the whole thing (as a viewer, not as the editor who's seen every cut already)
        ↓
16. Cut anything unnecessary (a second, harder pass)
        ↓
17. Check the audio (Module 11/12 — voice clarity, balance, headphones AND phone speaker)
        ↓
18. Check the pacing again (Module 16 — did earlier fixes hold up once everything else was added?)
        ↓
19. Watch it cold, like a first-time viewer (does the hook work — Module 17?)
        ↓
20. Send the first draft (with notes on judgment calls — Module 19)
        ↓
21. Get feedback (Feedback Template — Module 19)
        ↓
22. Make changes (Revision Checklist — Module 19)
        ↓
23. Final quality check (Module 21's checklist + Final Approval Checklist)
        ↓
24. Export (Module 6's export settings, saved using Module 1's naming/folder pattern)
```

## Why this order matters

- **Steps 1–5 (understanding and tagging) happen before real cutting starts.** Skipping straight to cutting is the most common beginner mistake — you miss a better structure that only becomes obvious once you've seen the whole recording, and you can waste time on the wrong moments early.
- **Step 6 (the rough cut) is about structure only.** Get the story's shape right (Modules 13/14/18) before any polish. Adding zooms and sound to a sequence you're about to reorder just wastes work that gets thrown away later.
- **Steps 7–8 (cutting boring parts, fixing pacing) happen before comedy and visuals (9–14).** Pacing problems need footage-level fixes — cutting, reordering, speeding up — that get much harder once memes, zooms, and sounds are already synced to specific frames that might move.
- **Steps 15–19 are a full-video review**, separate from building. Problems invisible while you're working on one moment at a time — a slow overall pace, an overused meme, an audio issue — only show up watching the whole thing straight through, like a viewer would.
- **Steps 20–23 close the loop with the creator** (Module 19). A video isn't done when the editor thinks it's done — it's done once it's checked against the brief and approved.

## If you're short on time

This process doesn't always go in a perfectly straight line — sometimes step 15 reveals a step 7 problem you missed, and that's fine, go back. But the **order of priority stays fixed**: fix structure and pacing problems before polishing the comedy layer further, every time you find one.

If something has to give under a deadline, cut time from later polish steps first (step 14, text/captions, is the most skippable if truly necessary) — before cutting time from steps 1–8. A well-structured, well-paced video with lighter polish beats a heavily polished video with structural problems.
