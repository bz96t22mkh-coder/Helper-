# Module 20 — Complete Editing Workflow

This is the full process, start to finish, that everything in Modules 1–19 feeds into. Print this or keep it open as a reference until it's second nature.

```
 1. Receive footage
        ↓
 2. Receive title / thumbnail / video concept (Editor Brief — Module 19)
        ↓
 3. Understand the video's story (Module 18 — what is this trying to make the viewer feel?)
        ↓
 4. Watch the footage (full watch-through, take timestamp notes — Module 13/14 tagging)
        ↓
 5. Identify important / funny moments (tag: funny, important, reaction, joke, story, suspense, boring, repetitive, unexpected)
        ↓
 6. Create the rough cut (assemble the spine — the story's throughline, in order, unpolished)
        ↓
 7. Remove boring footage (Module 16)
        ↓
 8. Fix pacing (Module 16 — rhythm, not just speed)
        ↓
 9. Add comedic cuts (Module 8)
        ↓
10. Add zooms (Module 9)
        ↓
11. Add memes / reaction images (Module 10)
        ↓
12. Add sound effects (Module 11)
        ↓
13. Add music (Module 12)
        ↓
14. Add text / captions where useful (Module 6)
        ↓
15. Review the entire video (full watch-through, as a viewer, not as the editor who's seen every cut already)
        ↓
16. Remove anything unnecessary (a second, more critical pass — things that felt fine in isolation while building sometimes don't survive a full watch)
        ↓
17. Check audio (Module 11/12 — voice clarity, music/SFX balance, on headphones AND a phone speaker)
        ↓
18. Check pacing (Module 16 — did earlier fixes hold up once everything else was layered in?)
        ↓
19. Watch it as a viewer (cold — imagine seeing this channel for the first time; does the hook work — Module 17?)
        ↓
20. Send first draft (with judgment-call notes — Module 19)
        ↓
21. Receive feedback (Feedback Template — Module 19)
        ↓
22. Make revisions (Revision Checklist — Module 19)
        ↓
23. Final quality check (Module 21's Quality Checklist + Final Approval Checklist)
        ↓
24. Export (Module 6's export settings, filed per Module 1's naming/folder convention)
```

## Why this order matters

- **Steps 1–5 (understanding and tagging) happen entirely before real cutting starts.** Skipping straight to cutting without a full watch-through is the single most common beginner mistake — it leads to missing a better structural option that only becomes obvious once you've seen the whole recording, and it leads to over-investing in the wrong moments early.
- **Step 6 (rough cut) is structure only** — get the story's shape right (Module 13/14/18) before any polish. Adding zooms, memes, and sound to a sequence you're about to restructure wastes the exact work that gets thrown away when things get reordered.
- **Steps 7–8 (remove boring, fix pacing) happen before the comedic/visual layer (9–14) is added**, because pacing problems are structural — they need footage-level fixes (cutting, reordering, speed) that get much harder to make once memes, zooms, and sound effects are already synced to specific frames that might move.
- **Steps 15–19 are a full-video review pass**, separate from the building process, because problems that are invisible while working on individual moments (overall pacing drag, an overused meme, a mix issue) only become obvious watching the whole thing straight through as a viewer would.
- **Steps 20–23 close the loop with the creator** (Module 19) — a video isn't done when the editor thinks it's done, it's done when it's been reviewed against the brief and approved.

## Practical notes

- This isn't strictly linear in practice — you'll sometimes jump back (a Step 15 review reveals a Step 7 pacing issue you missed) — but the *order of priority* stays fixed: structure and pacing problems get fixed before polishing the comedic layer further, every time you find one.
- On a tight deadline, if something has to be cut short, cut time from later polish steps (14, text/captions being the most skippable if genuinely necessary) before cutting time from steps 1–8 — a well-structured, well-paced video with lighter polish outperforms a heavily polished video with structural/pacing problems.
