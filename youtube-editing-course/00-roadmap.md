# YouTube Gaming + Reaction Editing Course — Roadmap

**Start here.** This file is the map for the whole course: what you'll learn, in what order, and why. Read it once fully before starting Module 1.

## Who this is for

You are starting from **zero editing experience**. This course assumes you don't know what a timeline is yet. It ends with you being able to take raw gaming or reaction footage and independently cut, pace, meme, sound-design, and export a finished YouTube video with a clear comedic identity — no hand-holding required.

It does **not** try to make you a professional film editor. Color science, advanced compositing, multi-cam broadcast workflows — none of that is here, because none of it is what makes a Gaming + Reaction channel entertaining. Everything in this course earns its place because it directly helps you make funnier, more watchable YouTube videos, inspired by the pacing and comedic energy of creators like CoryxKenshin, while building toward our own recognizable style.

## How the course is taught (read this before Module 1)

- **One skill at a time.** Each module teaches one thing deeply instead of many things shallowly. Don't skip ahead to "advanced" modules before finishing the fundamentals — the later modules assume you're comfortable with everything before them.
- **Every module ends in practice, not just theory.** Reading about a comedic zoom doesn't teach your hands to do it. Do the exercises. Module 22 has 10 full practice assignments, but most modules also have small drills you should do immediately.
- **Checklists and templates are tools you'll actually reuse**, not homework to file away. Module 21's quality checklist should live open on your second monitor every time you deliver a video.
- **Software and licensing information changes.** Where this course names a specific price, feature, or license term, treat it as "true as of when this was written" and re-check the source before you rely on it for a monetized upload. Module 3 teaches you exactly how to do that check yourself.

## Suggested pace

This isn't a race, but as a rough guide for a beginner working through it part-time:

- **Week 1:** Modules 1–5 (setup, software choice, legal basics, asset sources, library building) — no real editing yet, but this foundation prevents copyright strikes and wasted time later.
- **Week 2–3:** Modules 6–7 (learn the software for real, learn general YouTube pacing) plus Exercises 1–3 from Module 22.
- **Week 4:** Modules 8–12 (comedic technique, zooms, memes, sound, music) plus Exercises 4–6.
- **Week 5:** Modules 13–18 (gaming/reaction specifics, personality, retention, hooks, working from a plan) plus Exercise 7.
- **Week 6+:** Modules 19–23 (working with the creator, full workflow, checklist, style guide) plus Exercises 8–10, culminating in a real, published-quality video.

## The final goal

By the end of this course, given raw gaming or reaction footage, a title/thumbnail concept, and nothing else, you should be able to independently produce a polished, funny, personality-driven YouTube video that makes a viewer think *"this is so entertaining, I want to keep watching"* — with purposeful memes, well-timed zooms, satisfying sound design, strong pacing, and a clear sense of the creator's personality, all without it feeling over-edited or like a random meme collage.
