# YouTube Gaming + Reaction Editing Course — Roadmap

> **In short:** This page is your map. It shows the 24 modules in order, tells you how the course is taught, and tells you what you'll be able to do by the end.

**Start here.** Read this whole page first. Then start Module 1.

## Who this course is for

You are starting from **zero editing experience**. That's okay. This course does not assume you already know what a "timeline" is.

By the end, you will be able to:
- Take raw gaming or reaction footage
- Cut it, pace it, add memes and sound
- Export a finished YouTube video
- Do all of this **on your own**, without help

The style you're learning is high-energy and funny. Think of the pacing and comedy style used by creators like CoryxKenshin. But the goal is not to copy anyone. The goal is to build our **own** style.

This course does **not** try to make you a professional film editor. You will not learn color science or complex studio workflows. You only learn what actually makes a Gaming + Reaction channel fun to watch.

## How this course is taught

Read this before you start Module 1.

- **One skill at a time.** Each module teaches one thing. Don't skip ahead. Later modules assume you already know the earlier ones.
- **Practice, not just reading.** Reading about a zoom does not teach your hands how to do it. Do the exercises. Module 22 has 10 full practice assignments. Many other modules have small practice tasks too.
- **Checklists and templates are tools.** Use them on real videos. Don't just read them once and forget them. Module 21's checklist should be open every time you finish a video.
- **Rules and prices can change.** Software prices and legal rules change over time. If this course names a specific price or rule, check it's still true before you trust it for a real, money-earning video. Module 3 teaches you exactly how to check.

## A simple pace to follow

There's no rush. Here's one way to pace yourself, part-time:

- **Week 1:** Modules 1–5. Set up your computer and folders, pick your software, learn the legal basics, and learn where to get assets. You won't do much real editing yet — but this step protects you from copyright problems later.
- **Weeks 2–3:** Modules 6–7. Learn the software for real. Learn general YouTube pacing. Do Exercises 1–3 from Module 22.
- **Week 4:** Modules 8–12. Learn comedy technique, zooms, memes, sound, and music. Do Exercises 4–6.
- **Week 5:** Modules 13–18. Learn gaming and reaction editing, personality, pacing, hooks, and working from a plan. Do Exercise 7.
- **Week 6 and on:** Modules 19–23. Learn to work with the creator, the full workflow, the checklist, and the style guide. Do Exercises 8–10. Finish with one real, upload-ready video.

## The finish line

By the end of this course, you should be able to do this:

Someone hands you raw gaming or reaction footage, a title, and a thumbnail idea. That's it — no other help. You should be able to turn that into a finished, funny, well-paced YouTube video that makes people think: **"This is so entertaining. I want to keep watching."**

The video should have memes, zooms, and sound effects — but only where they earn their place. It should feel clean and planned, not like a random pile of effects.
