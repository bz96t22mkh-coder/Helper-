# Module 23 — Channel Editing Style Guide

This is the last module, and it's different from the rest: it's not a lesson, it's a **living document template**. Copy the template below into its own file (e.g., `00-ASSETS-LIBRARY/channel-style-guide.md`) and update it as the channel grows and you both learn what the audience actually responds to. Revisit it roughly every 5–10 videos.

## Why this exists

Modules 8 and 9 were explicit: study creators like CoryxKenshin for the *principles* behind high-energy, personality-driven comedic editing — never to copy them frame-for-frame. This document is where that understanding turns into something that's actually **ours**: specific, recognizable choices that make this channel's videos identifiable even with the sound off, or in the first two seconds of a thumbnail-scroll.

The eventual target, stated plainly: **funny + energetic + clean + personality-driven + unpredictable + entertaining** — this document is where "how we specifically achieve that" gets written down and refined over time, instead of re-decided from scratch on every video.

## How to use it in practice

- **After every video, or every few videos**, spend five minutes updating the relevant sections based on what actually happened — audience response (comments, retention if available), and your own and the creator's honest sense of what landed.
- **Before starting a new video**, skim it — not as a rulebook to follow rigidly (Module 8's "unexpected edits" and this course's "unpredictable" goal both argue against rigid rules), but as a reminder of what's already been learned about this specific channel, so good instincts don't have to be rediscovered from zero every time.
- **Treat contradictions as data, not mistakes.** If something in "things that are specifically us" stops landing a few videos later, that's not a failure of the guide — audiences and the channel's own voice evolve; update the document rather than forcing consistency with an earlier version of the style that's moved on.

## Closing note

You started this course as a beginner who didn't know what a timeline was. By this point, you have: a working software setup, a legally sound and well-organized asset library, a full technical toolkit (cutting, zooms, sound, color, keyframes), the editing judgment to build pacing and comedy on purpose rather than by accident, genre-specific skill for both gaming and reaction content, a real communication system with the creator, and now — a way to keep refining all of it into something that's recognizably, specifically this channel's own. That's the whole goal: take raw footage and independently produce something a viewer watches and thinks, *"this is so entertaining, I want to keep watching."*
