# Module 15 — Editing the Creator's Personality

> **In short:** This is the most important module in the course. The creator's personality is the actual product. The edit's job is to protect it and make it bigger — never to bury it or replace it with borrowed jokes.

This module doesn't teach a new technical skill. It teaches you *what all the other tools are for*. Modules 6–14 are tools. This module is the purpose behind using them.

## What to look for

Watch raw footage specifically for these — as a separate pass from your Module 13/14 pass for gameplay and reaction moments:

- **Jokes** — the creator's own sense of humor, whatever it turns out to be. Protect the exact wording and timing, instead of cutting it so tight that it clips the joke's natural rhythm.
- **Facial expressions and reactions** — the specific ways this creator reacts. A signature face, a particular laugh. Learn to spot these fast. They're worth extra attention (Module 9's zoom, Module 8's exaggeration) because they're recognizable and specific to this creator.
- **Funny comments** — off-the-cuff lines that could get lost in a fast sequence. Give them room to be heard.
- **Stories** — if the creator goes on a tangent, judge it on whether it's engaging, not just whether it's "on topic." Personality-driven channels often do well *because* of these detours.
- **Emotional moments** — real excitement, frustration, or a sincere moment inside the comedy. These need **protecting**, not amplifying with the usual comedy toolkit (see below).
- **Personality quirks** — repeated phrases, catchphrases, specific ways of describing things. These become the channel's signature over time (Module 23). Learn to spot and keep them consistent, video after video.
- **Unexpected statements** — a surprising or unusually honest comment is often one of the most memorable moments in a video. Don't let it get lost in a busy, effects-heavy sequence.

## Your job: make it bigger, don't replace it

Here's the mistake this module exists to prevent: an editor who doesn't trust the raw footage is "enough," so they add memes, zooms, and sound effects to *everything* to compensate. The result is a generic, high-energy video that happens to feature this creator's voice — not a video that clearly belongs to this creator.

**The test:** if you removed every meme, zoom, and sound effect from a finished cut, would it still be clear whose channel this is? Would it still be a little entertaining, just from the creator alone? If not, the edit has drifted from "making the personality bigger" to "covering up a lack of it" — and the fix isn't more effects. The fix is going back to the raw footage and finding the personality moments you under-used.

## Protecting sincere moments

When a moment turns genuinely serious or sentimental — even briefly, in an otherwise funny video — the comedy toolkit (memes, punchy zooms, comedic sounds) should mostly step back. A meme dropped on a sincere moment reads as accidental, or worse, mocking. Instead:
- Let the moment run closer to real time. Don't compress it heavily.
- If you use music at all, use the "Sad-Sentimental" category (Module 12), not the comedy playlist.
- Simple, clean editing — a still frame, few cuts — says "this matters" better than any effect could.

## Build a personality note as you go

As you learn a creator's style across multiple videos, keep a running note (this becomes part of Module 23's style guide) of:
- Their repeated phrases and catchphrases.
- The type of humor that consistently lands, and the type that doesn't.
- Specific facial expressions or reactions worth recognizing on sight.

This turns "editing their personality well" from a vague feeling into something specific you get faster at, video after video.
