# Module 4 — Where to Get Assets

> **In short:** This is a checked list of safe places to get music, sound effects, memes, footage, images, and fonts — with clear notes on what's free, what needs credit, and what's risky.

Before using anything for a real, money-earning video: check its license on the source's own current website. Rules and prices listed here were true when this was written, but they can change. This module is the practical partner to Module 3's "how to check" steps.

**Quick key:** 🆓 Free · 💰 Paid · ✅ OK for a monetized YouTube video · ⚠️ Read the rules carefully · 📝 Credit required

| Source | What you get | Cost | Monetized OK? | Credit needed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube Audio Library** (inside YouTube Studio) | Music and sound effects | 🆓 | ✅ for most tracks | ⚠️ Some tracks need credit ("CC BY"). Others don't. Check the label on each track. | Most tracks can only be used **on YouTube**, not other platforms, unless marked CC BY. |
| **Epidemic Sound** | A huge, pre-checked music library made for creators | 💰 Subscription | ✅ Pre-cleared, and videos usually stay covered even after you cancel (check current terms) | 📝 Not needed | A very safe, popular choice once the channel can afford it. |
| **Artlist** | Music, sound effects, and footage in one subscription | 💰 Subscription | ✅ | 📝 Not needed | Similar to Epidemic Sound. Read their terms if the video will run TV ads, not just normal YouTube ads. |
| **NCS (NoCopyrightSounds)** | Free music made for gaming channels | 🆓 | ✅ for most tracks | 📝 Usually yes — check each track's own credit text | Check the license note on each track. Not every track follows the same rule. |
| **Pixabay Music** | Free tracks | 🆓 | ✅ | 📝 Not needed | Smaller library, but zero cost and no credit hassle. |

## 😂 Memes / Reaction Images

**This is the riskiest category. Read carefully.** Most viral meme pictures are built on a photo, screenshot, or movie frame — and that original image is usually copyrighted too. A meme being famous or "everywhere" does **not** mean it's legal to use. People have been sued over famous meme photos before. Treat any meme from a random image search as **unchecked**, not safe.

Safer options, best first:

1. **Make your own, from the creator's real footage.** Screenshot the creator's own reaction face, or a frame from the creator's own gameplay. You already own this — it's the channel's own content. This is genuinely the safest option, and Module 5 shows you how to build a library this way.
2. **Meme-maker websites with their own clear commercial rules** (like Imgflip). Some explain how their generated images can be used commercially. Read their current terms before trusting this.
3. **Creator libraries built for video** (like Envato Elements or Artlist's template library) that clearly license meme-style graphics for commercial video use.
4. **Avoid:** grabbing a meme straight from Google Images, Pinterest, Reddit, or a random post. There's no license attached, and no way to know who really owns it.

## Build your own reusable library

Once you've checked a source and it's safe, don't check it again every time. Save it once into your shared `00-ASSETS-LIBRARY/` folder (Module 1), sorted by category (Module 5 shows the exact layout). Keep a simple note of where it came from and what license it's under — for example, a `sources.txt` file in each folder:

```
Music/Comedy-Upbeat/sources.txt
---
bouncy-ukulele-loop.mp3 — Epidemic Sound, subscription license, no credit needed
retro-8bit-win.wav — Pixabay, no credit needed
```

This turns "is this safe to use?" from a question you ask every video, into a check you do once per asset — done right, and written down.
