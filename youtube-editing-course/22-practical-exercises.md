# Module 22 — Practical Training

> **In short:** Reading builds knowledge. Practice builds skill. Work through these 10 exercises in order — each one builds on the last.

Do these in order. Use real footage where you can, or realistic practice footage. For every exercise, check your own work against the feedback criteria before moving on. It's even better if the creator or another editor looks at it too.

## Exercise 1 — Edit 60 seconds of raw gaming footage

**Task:** Take 3–5 minutes of raw, unedited gaming footage. Cut it down to a tight 60 seconds that tells one small, complete story — a beginning, an attempt, and an outcome.

**Check your work:**
- Does the 60 seconds feel complete, not like an arbitrary chunk cut off mid-thought?
- Is there any boring, dead time left in (Module 16)?
- Are the cuts clean — no stray frames, no accidental jitter?

## Exercise 2 — Practice clean cuts

**Task:** Take one continuous clip. Practice 10 different cuts using the Blade tool and ripple delete (Module 6), each one removing a different small section, with no gap and no audio pop at the cut point.

**Check your work:**
- Is every cut seamless, unless it's meant to be a hard, noticeable cut?
- Any audio glitch — a pop, a cut-off word — at any cut point?
- Did ripple delete close every gap correctly?

## Exercise 3 — Practice comedic zooms

**Task:** Take five moments with different levels of intensity (same as Module 9's exercise). Apply a matching zoom to each — at least one subtle punch-in, one hard cut-zoom, and one slower dramatic zoom.

**Check your work:**
- Does each zoom's size match its moment's size (Module 8's pairing table)?
- Is the timing locked to the beat of the moment — not early, not late?
- Does any zoom look blurry or pixelated from zooming in too far (Module 9)?

## Exercise 4 — Practice sound effects

**Task:** Take one minute of footage with at least five clear moments. Add a matching sound effect from your Module 5 library to each, using a different category each time.

**Check your work:**
- Is each sound synced exactly to its visual moment?
- Does the volume of each sound match the size of its moment, instead of staying the same throughout?
- With your eyes closed (Module 11's test), does the sequence feel satisfying, not cluttered?

## Exercise 5 — Practice meme timing

**Task:** Take one minute of footage. Find 2–3 real meme-worthy moments (not more — Module 10's rule). Place a matching meme on each, with the right timing, length, and placement.

**Check your work:**
- Does each meme match the specific feeling of its moment?
- Is it placed well — not blocking important content, readable at small size?
- Would each moment still be a little funny without the meme (Module 10's test)?

## Exercise 6 — Edit a funny reaction

**Task:** Take 2–3 minutes of raw reaction footage. Edit it into something tight and engaging, using Module 14's rules — emphasizing the reaction, cutting the dead "just watching" stretches.

**Check your work:**
- Is there any stretch where the viewer is just watching the source content with no added value (Module 14's core test)?
- Are the strongest reactions given the right amount of attention?
- Does the pacing avoid both boredom and exhaustion (Module 16)?

## Exercise 7 — Turn boring footage into something engaging

**Task:** Pick the least eventful, most repetitive part of your raw footage — menu navigation, walking, a repeated failed attempt. Edit it into something watchable, using compression, speed ramps, pattern interrupts, or reordering — without showing anything that didn't really happen.

**Check your work:**
- Is the result honestly more watchable than the raw footage?
- Which technique from Module 16 did you use, and was it the right tool for this specific type of boring?
- Does it still show accurately what actually happened?

## Exercise 8 — Edit a complete 3–5 minute gaming video

**Task:** Take a full gaming footage session. Produce a complete, polished 3–5 minute video: the full workflow (Module 20), a real hook (Module 17), the comedy layer (Modules 8–12), and a full pass with the Module 21 checklist.

**Check your work:** Run the full Module 21 checklist. Also ask:
- Does the video have a clear spine — a real story (Module 13)?
- Does the first 30 seconds pass the "cold viewer" test (Module 17)?
- Would you actually send this to a creator for feedback, or does it still feel like practice?

## Exercise 9 — Edit a complete reaction video

**Task:** Same as Exercise 8, but for a full reaction video, using Module 14's rules throughout.

**Check your work:** Same Module 21 checklist, plus Module 14's "is the value coming from the creator?" test applied to the whole video, not just one stretch.

## Exercise 10 — Edit a complete YouTube video, start to finish

**Task:** Using real footage and an Editor Brief (Module 19, filled out by the creator, or as realistically as you can), run the entire Module 20 process end to end — including a real feedback round using the templates, revisions, and a Final Approval Checklist pass.

**Check your work:** This is the final exercise — check it against Modules 7, 8, 16, and 21 together, plus:
- Did you actually use the communication system (Module 19), or skip it under time pressure?
- Looking back, where did you spend the most time, and does that match what Module 20 says the priority should be (structure and pacing before polish)?
- Is this a video you'd be proud to have your name on as the editor?
