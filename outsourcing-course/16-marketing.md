### DAY 92 — MARKETING CHANNEL OVERVIEW & PRIORITY RANKING

**Module 16: Marketing — Lesson 1**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Understand exactly which marketing channels matter for this business and why, ranked in the order you should actually invest time and money in them — so the next five days build in the right order instead of chasing whatever seems trendy.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. The ranking, and why it's ranked this way for this specific business.**

| Rank | Channel | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **WhatsApp + referrals** | Your primary sales channel already (Day 1), and free — a happy customer's referral to another security/cleaning company owner is the highest-trust, lowest-cost lead source that exists for this niche |
| 2 | **Google Business Profile** | Free, and this is how a prospect who's heard your name (from a referral, or found you searching) verifies you're real and credible before replying to a message |
| 3 | **LinkedIn** | The actual decision-makers (owners/ops managers of SME security and cleaning companies) are reachable there, and organic content/outreach costs nothing but time |
| 4 | **Paid ads** | Optional, lower priority, tested cautiously and only once the free channels are working — this niche's buyers are found more reliably through direct relationship and search-when-needed than through cold ad targeting |

**2. Why this order, not a generic "marketing funnel."** Generic marketing advice often starts with paid acquisition. This business doesn't, because its buyer persona (an SME owner/ops manager, reachable directly, in a market where reputation travels fast within a fragmented industry) makes relationship-driven and findability-driven channels far more efficient than paid reach. This isn't "ads don't work" — it's "for this specific niche, at this specific stage, they're not where the leverage is yet."

**3. What "systemizing" a channel means, this module.** You already have relationships, a website, and some organic activity from earlier modules. This module isn't starting from zero — it's turning ad-hoc activity (posting when you remember, asking for a referral only when it occurs to you) into a repeatable process you run every week without having to think about whether to do it.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

Priority order doesn't mean "only do #1, then #2, etc. in sequence over months" — it means when you have limited time, spend it on #1 and #2 before #3, and don't spend real money on #4 until the free channels are producing real, trackable results. All four can run in parallel; the ranking is about where your limited hours and rands go first when something has to give.

### DAY 93 — GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE SETUP

**Module 16: Marketing — Lesson 2**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Set up (or properly complete) a Google Business Profile that makes your business look credible and findable the moment a prospect searches for it — most of this is a one-time setup with a small ongoing habit.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Step-by-step setup:**
1. Go to Google Business Profile (business.google.com) and claim or create your listing using the business name and details finalized in Module 7 (Branding).
2. **Category**: choose the closest accurate category (e.g. "Clothing wholesaler" or "Uniform supplier," whichever Google's own list offers closest to your actual business) — this is what determines which searches you show up in.
3. **NAP consistency**: your business Name, Address, and Phone number must match exactly everywhere they appear (website, WhatsApp Business profile, any directory listings) — inconsistency between listings actively hurts how much Google trusts and ranks your profile.
4. **Service area**: set this to the real region(s) you actually serve (from your Day 1 home-city/region decision), so you show up for local searches without misleading anyone outside your real delivery range.
5. **Description**: write 2-3 sentences using the actual language a prospect would search — "branded uniforms and workwear for security and cleaning companies," your city/region, embroidery/print, delivery. Plain and accurate, not keyword-stuffed.
6. **Photos**: upload real photos of actual delivered work (with customer permission where the branding is customer-specific) — branded shirts, packaging, your process. This is often what actually converts a profile visit into a message, more than the text does.
7. **Verification**: complete whatever verification method Google offers (often a postcard, phone, or email depending on your account) — an unverified listing carries far less trust and may not show fully in search.

**2. The small ongoing habit.** Once live, occasionally post an update (a recent delivery, a new product line) and — critically — respond to any review, good or bad, promptly and professionally (tying back to the LAFF framework from Module 14 if a review is a complaint). A profile that's clearly active is more trusted than one that was set up once and abandoned.

**3. Reviews tie forward to Day 95.** Asking satisfied customers for a Google review is part of the referral system you'll systemize on Day 95 — don't build a separate process for it later; it's the same ask.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

A Google Business Profile doesn't generate leads by itself the way an ad does — its job is to be the credibility check a prospect runs after hearing about you some other way (a referral, a LinkedIn message, a cold call). An incomplete or inconsistent profile at that moment can quietly lose you a prospect who was already interested, which is why this is ranked #2, not an afterthought.

### EXERCISES

1. **Draft your profile description** (2-3 sentences, per the guidance above) before you type it into Google. A strong answer names your actual niche (security and cleaning companies), your real service area, and what you offer (branded uniforms, embroidery/print) — in the plain language a real prospect would use, not marketing jargon.
2. **List what photos you actually have available right now** to upload, and what you're missing. A strong answer is honest about gaps (e.g. "I don't have a good photo of a finished delivery yet") so you know what to capture on your next real order.

2. **[ME]** Confirm NAP consistency across your website, WhatsApp Business, and this new listing.
3. **[ME]** Upload whatever real photos you have now, and note what to capture on your next delivery (ties to Day 77's delivery photo habit from Module 13).

### DAY 94 — LINKEDIN PRESENCE & OUTREACH CONTENT PLAN

**Module 16: Marketing — Lesson 3**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Set up a credible LinkedIn presence and a light, sustainable content and outreach plan aimed at the real decision-makers in security and cleaning companies — without turning it into a second full-time job.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Personal profile vs. company page — use both, lightly.** Your personal profile is where direct outreach and relationship-building happens (people respond to people, not logos); a company page gives you a credible destination to point prospects to and a place to repost content. Set up or tidy both, but don't overinvest in the company page at the expense of your personal activity — for a business this size, personal outreach and posting does the actual work.

**2. Content plan, kept light — a manageable cadence.** Aim for roughly 2 posts a week, alternating between:
- **Educational/practical posts**: something a security or cleaning company owner would find genuinely useful — e.g. why consistent branded uniforms matter for a security company's professionalism with clients, how staff turnover affects uniform planning, a simple explainer on embroidery vs. print.
- **Proof-of-work posts**: a real (permission-granted) example of a delivered order, described plainly — what was needed, what you delivered, without fabricated statistics or invented client names/testimonials.

**3. Outreach, not cold pitching.** Connection requests to relevant people (ops managers/owners at security and cleaning companies you've identified, e.g. from your Module 2 prospect research) should include a short, genuine note — not an immediate sales pitch. A reasonable connection note: "Hi [Name], I work with security and cleaning companies in [region] on branded uniforms — noticed [something specific and true about their company/post], would be good to connect." The actual sales conversation happens after connection, via your Module 9 process, not in the first message.

**4. Time budget.** This fits inside your existing 2-hour days — a realistic weekly LinkedIn budget is 30-45 minutes total (writing/posting + a handful of genuine outreach messages), not a daily open-ended scroll.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

LinkedIn content for this niche isn't about going viral — it's about a small, real number of the right people (ops managers and owners in your actual market) seeing you show up consistently and credibly over months, so that when they do need uniforms, or a referral comes their way, your name is already familiar. Consistency at a light, sustainable cadence beats an intense burst you can't keep up.

### EXERCISES

1. **Draft 2 real post ideas** — one educational, one proof-of-work (using a real order you've delivered, with permission, or a realistic placeholder if you don't have one yet noted as such). A strong answer avoids fabricated statistics or invented customer quotes; if you don't have a real example yet, the exercise answer should say so honestly rather than inventing one.
2. **Draft a real connection-request note** for a specific prospect from your existing prospect list. A strong answer references something genuinely specific to that company, not a copy-pasted generic line.

### DAY 95 — SYSTEMIZING WHATSAPP & REFERRAL MARKETING

**Module 16: Marketing — Lesson 4**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Turn your highest-value, lowest-cost channel — referrals from happy customers, delivered mostly over WhatsApp — from something you do when you remember into a repeatable step built into your actual fulfilment process.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Why this is ranked #1, worth repeating.** A referral from a security company owner to another security company owner (or a cleaning company owner — the buyer persona is nearly identical per the roadmap) arrives pre-trusted in a way no ad or cold outreach can match, and it costs you nothing but the moment it takes to ask.

**2. When to ask.** The best moment is right after a successful delivery and positive follow-up (Day 79's reorder check-in touchpoint, or right after a smooth first delivery) — when satisfaction is highest and freshest in the customer's mind, not months later out of nowhere.

**3. A simple, direct referral script:**

> "Glad these worked out well for you. If you know anyone else — another security or cleaning company, even in a different area — who might need something similar, I'd really appreciate the introduction. No pressure at all, just always looking to work with good people like yourself."

Keep it low-pressure and genuine — this isn't a hard ask, and treating it as one undermines the relationship you're trying to leverage.

**4. Systemizing it — build the ask into the process, not your memory.** This connects directly to the fulfilment pipeline (Module 13) and the reorder-trigger SOP (Day 79): the referral ask becomes a defined step at "Follow-Up Sent" in your order tracker, not something you have to remember to think of separately. The Google Business Profile review ask (Day 93) can ride along in the same message.

**5. On incentives — optional, and margin-disciplined.** A small incentive (e.g. a modest discount on the referring customer's next order) can help, but isn't required — this niche's referrals often work fine on relationship alone. If you do offer one, make sure it's built into your Module 6 pricing so it doesn't quietly erode your margin; never discount ad-hoc without checking the numbers.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

Systemizing a free channel is still real work — the value here isn't automation, it's discipline: making sure the ask genuinely happens every time satisfaction is highest, instead of only when you happen to remember. That consistency is what turns an occasional lucky referral into a real, repeatable lead source.

### EXERCISES

1. **Write your own version of the referral-ask script**, in your own voice. A strong answer stays low-pressure and specific to your real relationship style — not a hard sell.
2. **Decide where exactly this ask fits into your real fulfilment process** (e.g. combined with the delivery follow-up, or the Day 79 reorder check-in). A strong answer names the specific trigger point, not "sometime after delivery."

### DAY 96 — TESTING A SMALL PAID-ADS BUDGET, CAUTIOUSLY

**Module 16: Marketing — Lesson 5**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Understand how to test a small paid-ads budget the right way — measuring real ROI on a genuinely small spend — while being honest that this channel is optional and lower priority than the three you've already built out.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Why this is last, and optional.** The roadmap's own expense guidance puts a marketing/ads test budget at roughly **R500-R2,000/month**, explicitly "once you have something to sell" — this is a test, not a growth strategy, and for this niche the free channels (Days 93-95) are doing more of the real work. If your free channels aren't yet running consistently, that's where more time belongs before ad spend, not instead of it.

**2. If you do test — what "cautious" actually means.**
- Start at the low end of the R500-R2,000 range, not the top.
- Pick one platform and one narrow goal (e.g. driving traffic to your Google Business Profile or website from a tightly targeted local audience), not a broad multi-platform campaign.
- Set a hard stop — decide the test budget and duration (e.g. R500 over 2 weeks) before you start, and don't extend it mid-test just because it "might work with a bit more."

**3. Measuring ROI on the test.** The only number that matters is: what did this spend actually produce, in real leads or conversations, compared to what it cost? Track this through your CRM's lead-source field (already built in Module 11) — tag every lead that came from this specific test so you can honestly answer "did R500 of ad spend produce X real leads, and did any convert?" A test that produces zero trackable leads is a real, useful result — it tells you not to spend more here yet, not a failure to hide.

**4. Being honest with yourself about the outcome.** It's tempting to justify ad spend after the fact ("well, it probably helped brand awareness"). Hold yourself to the actual number: leads generated, cost per lead, and (if enough time has passed) conversions — compared honestly to what the same R500-R2,000 would have bought in extra hours on WhatsApp/referral or LinkedIn outreach.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

A small, well-measured ad test either gives you real evidence to scale a channel deliberately, or real evidence to leave it alone and focus fully on the channels that are already working for this niche — either outcome is useful, as long as the test was genuinely small and genuinely measured. What's not useful is an unmeasured ad spend that neither proves nor disproves anything.

### EXERCISES

1. **Design your own small ad test** on paper before spending anything: platform, budget, duration, target audience, and the single goal it's measuring. A strong answer keeps the budget at the low end (around R500), the duration short (1-2 weeks), and the goal specific and trackable (e.g. "leads captured via the website contact form, tagged by source").
2. **Decide your stop/continue rule in advance.** A strong answer states a concrete threshold (e.g. "if cost per lead is meaningfully worse than what my organic channels are producing, I stop and don't repeat it without changing something specific") rather than a vague "see how it goes."

### BUSINESS IMPLEMENTATION

2. **[ME]** If proceeding, design and run your small test (from the exercise), tagging every resulting lead by source in the CRM.
3. **[ME]** Review the result honestly against your predefined stop/continue rule once the test period ends.

### TODAY'S DELIVERABLES
