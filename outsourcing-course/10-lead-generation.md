### DAY 53 — Lead Sources for This Niche

**Module 10: Lead Generation — Lesson 1**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Map every realistic source of new security and cleaning company leads available to you in South Africa, so your pipeline never depends on a single channel running dry.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Why relying on one source is risky.** A pipeline fed from only one channel (say, only Google Maps searches) plateaus once you've worked through the obvious results in your area. A healthy pipeline draws from several sources at once, so when one slows down, others keep it moving.

**2. The real lead sources available for this niche:**

- **Google Maps** — search terms like "security company" or "cleaning services" plus your city/region; gives you business names, often phone numbers, sometimes websites, and review counts (a rough size/activity signal). This is the same method your Day 1 prospect list already used — today formalizes and scales it.
- **PSIRA registry** — the Private Security Industry Regulatory Authority maintains a public register of registered security service providers in South Africa; this is a direct, niche-specific source of real, licensed security companies, often including ones that don't show up well on Google Maps.
- **Local business directories** — regional or municipal business directories and chamber-of-commerce-style listings often carry SMEs that don't invest much in an online presence but are exactly your target buyer.
- **Referrals** — your fastest-closing, highest-trust source once you have even one paying customer; a satisfied owner in this industry usually knows several peers in the same or adjacent industry (security-company owners tend to know other security-company owners). Ask for this explicitly (Module 17 covers this properly later, but start noting willing customers now).
- **LinkedIn** — useful for finding named decision-makers (owners, operations managers) at companies you've already identified elsewhere, and for a more formal first-contact channel when a phone number isn't available.
- **Drive-by prospecting** — physically noticing security guards or cleaning staff in branded uniforms while out and about (at malls, business parks, estates) and noting the company name off their uniform or vehicle branding, then looking them up. Unusually effective in this niche specifically, because the product you sell is worn visibly in public.

**3. How to prioritize your effort.** Not all sources cost the same time per lead. Google Maps and PSIRA are fast, systematic, and good for volume. Referrals are slow to start but nearly free and high-quality. Drive-by prospecting is opportunistic — cheap to do alongside normal life, not something to block dedicated hours for. Spend your structured research time on Maps/PSIRA/directories, and treat referrals and drive-by as an ongoing habit rather than a scheduled task.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

The point of listing six sources isn't to use all of them equally every day — it's to make sure that when one runs dry (you've exhausted the obvious Google Maps results in your area, say), you have others ready instead of concluding the market is smaller than it actually is. This niche, per the roadmap, is large and fragmented — a slow week of one source is a signal to switch sources, not a signal that leads have run out.

### DAY 54 — Organizing Leads: Structure and Scoring

**Module 10: Lead Generation — Lesson 2**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Design a lead-scoring framework so your growing prospect list tells you who to call first, instead of treating every row the same.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Why your Day 1 prospect-list.md needs to evolve.** It was built as a simple starter tracker — company name, industry, contact info, size, notes, status. That's enough to start finding prospects, but not enough to prioritize dozens or hundreds of them once volume grows. Today you design (and tomorrow, Day 56, Claude Code builds) the upgrade: real scoring fields and follow-up-due tracking.

**2. What a lead-scoring framework is.** A simple, consistent way of ranking leads by how promising they are, using a small number of factors you can actually assess quickly for each prospect — not a complex formula, just enough structure to answer "who do I contact first today" without guessing.

**3. Good scoring factors for this specific niche:**
- **Staff size** — bigger companies mean bigger orders and more frequent turnover-driven reorders; a very small company (a handful of guards) may be below a size worth prioritizing early.
- **Current supplier status** — no supplier or an informal one (higher opportunity, lower switching friction) vs. a clearly established formal supplier relationship (lower near-term opportunity, worth a lower-priority "check back later" approach).
- **Responsiveness** — has this prospect replied to any contact yet? A prospect who's replied once, even briefly, is meaningfully warmer than one who's never responded.
- **Fit signals** — visible in-industry signals (PSIRA registration status, a real physical presence, an active-looking business) vs. weak/unclear signals.

**4. A simple scoring method that works without a complex tool**: score each factor low/medium/high (or 1-3), sum them into a total, and use that total to sort your list into priority tiers (e.g. hot, warm, cold) — simple enough to fill in by hand today and by Claude Code's upgraded tracker tomorrow.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

A scoring framework is only useful if it's fast to apply — if scoring a lead takes longer than it would to just call them, you've over-engineered it. Keep every factor to something you can assess in seconds from what you already know or can glance up, not something requiring a new research task per lead.

### EXERCISES

3. **Sort them into hot/warm/cold tiers** based on the resulting scores, and note which tier you'll work first.

A strong scoring framework produces a ranking you actually agree with intuitively when you look at the results — if your framework says a company you know is a great fit scores "cold," recheck your factor definitions, not just that one row.

### DAY 55 — Designing a Follow-Up Cadence

**Module 10: Lead Generation — Lesson 3**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Design a concrete follow-up cadence — fixed touchpoints after first contact — so no lead you've put effort into goes cold simply because you forgot to check back in.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. Why most leads are lost to neglect, not rejection.** In practice, far more prospects go quiet from a busy owner simply not getting back to a first message than from an actual firm no. A single follow-up message often re-opens a conversation that looked dead. A fixed cadence turns this from something you have to remember into something the system reminds you to do.

**2. A proven simple cadence structure**: touchpoints at day 0, day 3, day 7, and day 14 after first contact, then a longer-interval "check back in a few months" touch if there's still no response by day 14 — rather than either giving up after one message or pestering daily.

- **Day 0** — first contact (your Day 47 cold outreach script).
- **Day 3** — a short, low-pressure follow-up if no reply (not repeating the same message — a brief "just checking this reached you" or adding one new piece of value, like a relevant point about your catalogue).
- **Day 7** — another light touch, possibly a different channel than Day 0/3 (e.g. if you WhatsApped first, try a call or email now).
- **Day 14** — a final near-term touch, often framed as "I'll leave this with you, feel free to reach out whenever" — closing the immediate loop without sounding like you're giving up on the relationship entirely.
- **Beyond day 14** — move to a long-interval "warm nurture" list (e.g. revisit in 2-3 months) rather than deleting the prospect — a "not right now" (Day 50) is often genuinely just about timing.

**3. Why each follow-up needs to say something slightly different.** Repeating the identical message three times reads as either automated or careless. Vary the angle slightly each time — a new relevant detail, a different question, a different channel — while staying just as short and low-pressure as the first message.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

A cadence only works if it's actually followed — which is exactly why Day 56 builds follow-up-due dates directly into your lead tracker instead of relying on memory. Today's job is designing the right intervals and message variations; tomorrow's job is making sure the system surfaces "who's due today" automatically so the cadence isn't something you have to hold in your head.

### EXERCISES

2. **Decide your channel-switching rule** — which channel you default to at each touchpoint, and when you'd switch (e.g. WhatsApp for 0/3, phone call attempt at 7, email at 14).
3. **Write your "beyond day 14" nurture message** — the one that closes the near-term loop gracefully and sets up a longer-term check-in.

A strong cadence reads, across all four messages, like a patient, professional person following up naturally — not like four escalating reminders that a message hasn't been answered.

A batch that's logged carelessly (missing outcomes, inconsistent dates) gives you nothing to learn from; a batch logged precisely, even if the results are modest, gives you your first real evidence of what's working and what isn't in your actual market, which is worth more at this stage than a lucky single close.

### DAY 58 — Capstone: Reviewing Real Results and Refining the Approach

**Module 10: Lead Generation — Lesson 6**

### TODAY'S OBJECTIVE

Review yesterday's real outreach numbers honestly, identify one or two concrete changes worth making based on what actually happened, and close out Module 10.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO LEARN

**1. How to read your own real conversion numbers without over- or under-reacting.** A single batch of 20-30 is a small sample — don't declare a channel or script "broken" or "great" off one day's data alone. Look instead for directional signals: which channel got more replies, which script version (if you varied any) landed better, which lead-scoring tier actually performed as expected.

**2. What to actually calculate from yesterday's log:**
- **Contact rate** — how many of your batch did you actually manage to reach (vs. no valid number/no response mechanism at all)?
- **Reply rate** — of those contacted, how many responded at all, positive or negative?
- **Positive-response rate** — of those who replied, how many moved toward a discovery call?
- **By tier** — did your hot-tier leads actually convert better than warm-tier? If not, that's a signal your Day 54 scoring factors may need adjusting.
- **By channel** — did WhatsApp outperform phone or email for you, specifically, today?

**3. What "refining the approach" means practically.** Not a full rebuild — one or two specific, evidence-based changes: maybe your Day 0 WhatsApp script needs a tighter opening line if reply rates were low, maybe your scoring framework's "fit signal" factor isn't actually predictive and needs reweighting, maybe a particular source (Day 53) produced noticeably weaker-quality leads than another. Write these down as concrete next actions, not vague impressions.

### WHAT YOU NEED TO UNDERSTAND

Real numbers, even disappointing ones, are more valuable than a comfortable guess — this is the entire point of insisting on a real batch with real logging in Day 57 instead of a hypothetical exercise. A 10% reply rate you know for certain beats an assumed "probably fine" you never actually measured, because only the measured number gives you something real to improve.
